Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "molecules in solids are packed more tightly together" → Answer: "molecules in solids are more closely packed"
Passage: "biodiversity changes rather than simply declines" → Answer: "changes the composition of species rather than eliminating all wildlife"
2 Understand passage structure
First sentence → Topic / Main claim
Middle → Details, examples, data
Last sentence → Conclusion / Summary

Main Idea questions can often be solved by reading just the first and last sentences.
Q1: Passage 1 opens with "Sound is a form of energy that travels through matter as a wave" and ends with "these properties explain why a whisper sounds different from a shout." Together these convey the main idea: the basic properties and behavior of sound waves = A.
3 Inference must come from the text only
Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions. Only information stated in the passage counts as evidence.
Most common mistake:
✓ Passage says "biodiversity changes rather than simply declines" → "urbanization changes composition of species" (valid inference from text)
✗ Passage mentions "urban adapters" → "most wildlife will eventually adapt" (unsupported generalization — the passage says only "some animals")
4 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the highlighted word with each option and see which one reads most naturally in the sentence.
Q5: "make populations more [exposed and at risk] to disease" reads naturally. "make populations more [resistant] to disease" means the opposite of what the passage describes.
The Physics of Sound Waves Physical Science
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A The basic properties and behavior of sound waves ✓
B Why sound cannot travel through outer space
C A comparison between sound waves and light waves
D How scientists measure the speed of sound in different environments
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 defines sound as a wave and explains how it propagates. Paragraph 2 discusses the speed of sound in different media. Paragraph 3 covers frequency and amplitude. All paragraphs describe properties and behavior of sound waves = A.

B is only a minor detail (sound needs a medium). C is wrong because light is mentioned only briefly for contrast, not as a main topic. D focuses on measurement methods, but the passage explains why speed differs, not how it is measured.
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "transmitted" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A absorbed
B passed along ✓
C reflected
D created
Context check: The sentence describes vibrations being "transmitted more quickly from one molecule to the next." Substituting: "vibrations [passed along] more quickly from one molecule to the next" reads naturally = B.

A (absorbed) suggests the vibrations are taken in and stopped. C (reflected) means bounced back, not forward. D (created) does not fit because the vibrations already exist and are being transferred.
Question 3 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, why does sound travel faster through solids than through gases?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A Solids contain fewer molecules that can interfere with the wave.
B Sound waves in solids have a higher frequency than in gases.
C The tighter arrangement of particles in solid materials allows energy to pass between them faster. ✓
D The amplitude of sound waves increases when passing through solid materials.
Explanation: Paragraph 2 explicitly states: "molecules in solids are packed more tightly together, allowing vibrations to be transmitted more quickly" = C.

A is the opposite — solids have more molecules packed closely, not fewer. B is unsupported — the passage does not say frequency changes with the medium. D confuses amplitude with speed.
Paraphrase: Passage: "packed more tightly together" → C: "more closely packed" — same meaning, different wording.
The Impact of Urbanization on Wildlife Social Science
Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention raccoons, pigeons, and hawks in paragraph 3?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A To argue that urbanization benefits all wildlife species equally
B To give examples of species that have successfully adapted to urban environments ✓
C To explain why these species are more genetically diverse than others
D To suggest that wildlife corridors are unnecessary for common species
Explanation: Paragraph 3 begins with "not all species are negatively affected" and then mentions raccoons, pigeons, and hawks as species that "have proven remarkably successful at exploiting urban environments." These are examples of successful urban adapters = B.

A uses the extreme word "all" — the passage says only "some" species benefit. C is not discussed in paragraph 3. D is from paragraph 4 and is not the purpose of these examples.
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "vulnerable" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A resistant
B indifferent
C exposed and at risk ✓
D attracted
Context check: The sentence discusses how isolation can "make populations more vulnerable to disease and environmental changes." Substituting: "make populations more [exposed and at risk] to disease" reads naturally = C.

A (resistant) is the opposite of vulnerable. B (indifferent) means not caring, which does not fit. D (attracted) makes no sense in context.
Question 6 — Inference
Based on the passage, what can be inferred about the relationship between urbanization and biodiversity?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A Urbanization always leads to a complete loss of biodiversity in affected areas.
B Species that can exploit human food waste are likely to thrive even as overall biodiversity declines. ✓
C Most wildlife species will eventually adapt to urban environments without assistance.
D Wildlife corridors have proven ineffective at protecting endangered species.
Text-based inference: Paragraph 3 states that urban adapters' success "raises important questions about how biodiversity changes rather than simply declines." This directly supports the idea that urbanization shifts species composition rather than eliminating all wildlife = B.

A uses "always" and "complete loss," which contradicts the passage (some species thrive). C overgeneralizes — the passage says "some animals," not "most." D contradicts paragraph 4, which says wildlife corridors "have shown promise."
Paraphrase: Passage: "biodiversity changes rather than simply declines" → B: "changes the composition of species rather than eliminating all wildlife"
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For inference questions, only the passage counts as evidence. Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions.
3. Main Idea = first sentence + last sentence. Do not get pulled into middle-paragraph details.
4. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
