Academic Reading 攻略 TipsAcademic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
この練習からの言い換え例:Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "molecules in solids are packed more tightly together" → Answer: "molecules in solids are more closely packed"
Passage: "biodiversity changes rather than simply declines" → Answer: "changes the composition of species rather than eliminating all wildlife"
2 Understand passage structure
First sentence → Topic / Main claim
Middle → Details, examples, data
Last sentence → Conclusion / Summary

Main Idea questions can often be solved by reading just the first and last sentences.
Q1: Passage 1 opens with "Sound is a form of energy that travels through matter as a wave" and ends with "these properties explain why a whisper sounds different from a shout." Together these convey the main idea: the basic properties and behavior of sound waves = A.
3 Inference must come from the text only
Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions. Only information stated in the passage counts as evidence.
最もよくある間違い:Most common mistake:
✓ Passage says "biodiversity changes rather than simply declines" → "urbanization changes composition of species" (valid inference from text)
✗ Passage mentions "urban adapters" → "most wildlife will eventually adapt" (unsupported generalization — the passage says only "some animals")
4 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the highlighted word with each option and see which one reads most naturally in the sentence.
Q7: "make populations more [susceptible] to disease" reads naturally. "make populations more [resistant] to disease" means the opposite of what the passage describes.

B is only a minor detail (sound needs a medium). C is wrong because light is mentioned only briefly for contrast, not as a main topic. D focuses on measurement methods, but the passage explains why speed differs, not how it is measured.

A 吸収されるabsorbed
B 運ばれる ✓carried ✓
C 反射されるreflected

文脈確認:Context check: 文は振動が「transmitted more quickly from one molecule to the next」と描写する。代入：「振動が分子から分子へ[伝えられて]より速く」自然に読める = B。

A (absorbed) suggests the vibrations are taken in and stopped. C (reflected) means bounced back, not forward. D (created) does not fit because the vibrations already exist and are being transferred.

設問 4 — 修辞目的Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
なぜ著者は第2段落で鋼と空気に言及するのか。Why does the author mention steel and air in paragraph 2?
正解: ACorrect: A あなたの回答: —Your answer: —
A 媒体によって音速がどれほど劇的に異なるかを示すため ✓To illustrate how dramatically the speed of sound differs between media ✓
B ほとんどの人が日常的に経験する唯一の媒体は空気だと証明するためTo prove that air is the only medium most people regularly experience
C 工業用防音に使われる異なる金属を比較するためTo compare different metals used for industrial soundproofing
D 空気中の湿気が音をかなり遅くすると示唆するためTo suggest that humidity in air slows sound down considerably
解説:Explanation: 第2段落は「sound moves fastest through solids, slower through liquids, and slowest through gases（音は固体で最速、液体ではより遅く、気体で最も遅い）」と主張し、その直後に「For example, sound travels through steel at approximately 5,960 meters per second, but through air at only about 343 meters per second」という対比を提示する。鋼（約 5,960 m/s）と空気（約 343 m/s）という具体例は、媒体間の速度差がいかに劇的かを読者に印象づける = A。

B は本文と無関係（本文は人々の経験には触れていない）。C は誤り。比較されているのは「金属同士」ではなく「固体 vs 気体」。D は本文にない要素（湿気）を持ち込んでいる。 Paragraph 2 claims that "sound moves fastest through solids, slower through liquids, and slowest through gases" and then immediately offers the contrast: "sound travels through steel at approximately 5,960 meters per second, but through air at only about 343 meters per second." The two concrete numbers (steel vs air) are given specifically to dramatize how large the difference between media can be = A.

B is unrelated — the passage does not discuss human experience. C compares metals to each other, but the passage actually contrasts a solid with a gas. D introduces "humidity," which the passage never mentions.
設問 5 — 推論Question 5 — Inference
第3段落に基づくと、同じ高さ（ピッチ）で振幅の異なる2つの音について何が推論できるか。Based on paragraph 3, what can be inferred about two sounds produced at the same pitch but different amplitudes?
正解: DCorrect: D あなたの回答: —Your answer: —
A 大きい方の音は静かな音より空気中を速く伝わるThe louder sound travels through air faster than the quieter sound
B 静かな音は大きな音より高い周波数で振動するThe quieter sound vibrates at a higher frequency than the loud one
C 両方の音はほとんどの人間の聴き手に同一に知覚されるBoth sounds will be perceived as identical by most human listeners
D より大きな振幅は、より小さな振幅よりも遠くからの検出を可能にする ✓Greater amplitude would allow detection from a greater distance than lesser amplitude ✓
本文に基づく推論:Text-based inference: 本文は「A sound wave with greater amplitude carries more energy and is perceived as louder by the human ear」と述べる。ここから直接「より多くのエネルギー」 = D とは答えられない（それは Factual）。より深い推論：エネルギーが多い → 音波が空気中を伝播する際の減衰を考慮すると、より遠くまで届く前に検出可能なレベルを保てる。つまり振幅が大きい音は、より遠くで検出される可能性がある = D。本文に直接書かれていない「距離での検出」を、振幅 → エネルギー → 伝播距離の鎖で推論する必要がある。

A は誤り。本文によれば速度は媒体で決まり、振幅で決まらない。B は周波数と振幅を混同。C は本文の主張と矛盾する。 The passage states "a sound wave with greater amplitude carries more energy and is perceived as louder." The correct answer requires a further inference: a wave carrying more energy must dissipate over a greater distance before falling below the listener's detection threshold — so greater amplitude allows detection from a greater range. The passage never directly mentions detection distance = D requires combining amplitude → energy → propagation range.

A is wrong: speed depends on the medium, not on amplitude. B confuses amplitude with frequency. C contradicts the passage.
推論ステップ:Inference step: "greater amplitude carries more energy" + sound dissipates as it travels → D: more energy allows detection from greater distance.

設問 8 — 推論Question 8 — Inference

重要ポイントKey Takeaways
1. 正解は常にパッセージの言い換えである。 パッセージと同じ単語を使った選択肢は罠であることが多い。
2. 推論問題では、根拠はパッセージ内のみ。 自分の知識や意見を持ち込まないこと。
3. 主旨 = 最初の文 + 最後の文。 段落中央の細部に引き込まれないように。
4. 文脈中の語彙：代入して確認。 各選択肢で語を置き換え、最も自然に読めるものを選ぶ。 1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For inference questions, only the passage counts as evidence. Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions.
3. Main Idea = first sentence + last sentence. Do not get pulled into middle-paragraph details.
4. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
