Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "fundamentally changed how goods were made and how people lived and worked" → Answer: "transformed society through urbanization, new social classes, and eventual improvements in living standards"
Passage: "the enzyme temporarily changes shape to fit around it more tightly" → Answer: "temporarily adjusts its shape to fit more closely around the substrate"
2 Understand passage structure
First sentence → Topic / Main claim
Middle → Details, examples, data
Last sentence → Conclusion / Summary

Main Idea questions can often be solved by reading just the first and last sentences of each paragraph.
Q1: Paragraph 1 introduces the Industrial Revolution. Paragraph 2 describes urbanization and new social classes. Paragraph 3 covers long-term improvements. The thread: how industrialization transformed society = A.
3 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the target word with each option and see which one reads most naturally in the sentence.
Q2: "One of the most [very deep and significant] effects of industrialization was the rapid growth of cities" reads naturally and matches the context of massive societal change. "Temporary" contradicts the lasting impact described; "slightly noticeable" is the opposite.
4 Rhetorical Purpose: ask "what point is the author making?"
When asked why the author mentions something, look at the sentence before the example. That sentence usually states the point the example is meant to illustrate.
Trap pattern: An option that accurately describes the example itself but misidentifies the author's purpose for including it.
The Industrial Revolution and Its Social Effects History
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A How the Industrial Revolution transformed society through urbanization, new social classes, and eventual improvements in living standards ✓
B Why steam engines were the most important invention in the history of technology
C The reasons why workers in the nineteenth century refused to move to cities
D A comparison between agricultural methods used before and after industrialization
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 introduces the Industrial Revolution and its shift from agriculture to factory production. Paragraph 2 describes urbanization and new social classes. Paragraph 3 discusses long-term improvements in living standards and labor reforms. The common thread across all three paragraphs is how industrialization transformed society = A.

B focuses on only one technology (steam engines) and uses extreme language ("most important"). C is directly contradicted by the passage, which says workers did migrate to cities. D is unsupported — the passage does not compare agricultural methods.
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "profound" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A temporary
B very deep and significant ✓
C slightly noticeable
D completely unexpected
Context check: The passage says "One of the most profound effects of industrialization was the rapid growth of cities," describing massive societal changes including overcrowding, new social classes, and urban migration. This context indicates deep and significant = B.

Substituting: "One of the most [very deep and significant] effects" reads naturally. A (temporary) contradicts the lasting changes described. C (slightly noticeable) is the opposite of the massive changes described. D (completely unexpected) has no support in the text.
Question 3 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention "overcrowded living conditions, poor sanitation, and the spread of disease" in paragraph 2?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A To illustrate the negative consequences of rapid urban growth during industrialization ✓
B To argue that rural life was always healthier than city life
C To explain why factory owners became wealthy
D To compare sanitation in Britain with sanitation in other European countries
Explanation: The sentence before the list states: "This mass movement of people led to overcrowded living conditions, poor sanitation, and the spread of disease." The author lists these problems to show the negative side effects of rapid urbanization = A.

B uses "always," which is too extreme and not the author's point. C discusses factory owners' wealth, which is a separate topic. D mentions a comparison between countries, which the passage never makes.
The Role of Enzymes in Biological Processes Life Science
Question 4 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, what happens when a substrate enters an enzyme's active site?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A The enzyme permanently changes into a different type of protein.
B The enzyme temporarily adjusts its shape to fit more closely around the substrate. ✓
C The substrate is immediately broken down into smaller molecules without any change in the enzyme.
D The active site releases energy that raises the temperature of the surrounding cells.
Explanation: Paragraph 2 states: "the enzyme temporarily changes shape to fit around it more tightly, a process scientists describe as induced fit." This directly matches B.

A says "permanently," but the passage says "temporarily." C claims no change occurs in the enzyme, which contradicts the shape change described. D mentions releasing energy to raise temperature, which is not stated anywhere in the passage.
Paraphrase: Passage: "temporarily changes shape to fit around it more tightly" → B: "temporarily adjusts its shape to fit more closely around the substrate"
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "induced" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A prevented
B random
C brought about or caused ✓
D inherited
Context check: The passage describes how the substrate entering the active site causes the enzyme to change shape — this is called "induced fit." The fit is brought about by the substrate's presence = C.

Substituting: "[brought about] fit" reads naturally, meaning the fit is caused by the substrate. A (prevented) is the opposite. B (random) contradicts the specific, substrate-triggered nature of the process. D (inherited) refers to genetic traits, not a chemical interaction.
Question 6 — Sentence Insertion
Look at the four squares in paragraph 3 that indicate where the following sentence could be added:

"Maintaining this precise balance of temperature and pH is essential because the enzyme’s three-dimensional shape depends on weak chemical bonds that are easily disrupted."
Correct: B Your answer: —
A After sentence 1 (after "...enzyme activity.")
B After sentence 2 (after "...and at a neutral pH level.") ✓
C After sentence 3 (after "...a process known as denaturation.")
D After sentence 4 (after "...loses its catalytic function entirely.")
Why B is correct (no discourse marker — content reasoning required): The insertion sentence mentions "this precise balance of temperature and pH." The demonstrative "this" requires a clear referent. Sentence 2 is the only sentence that specifies both the precise temperature (37°C) and the precise pH level (neutral). After sentence 2, "this precise balance" has a clear referent = B.

A is incorrect because sentence 1 is a general statement about environmental conditions; no specific temperature or pH values have been stated yet, so "this precise balance" has no referent. C is incorrect because by sentence 3, the paragraph has already moved to what happens when conditions deviate (denaturation); inserting a sentence about maintaining balance after the damage is already described would be illogical. D is incorrect for the same reason — the enzyme has already lost function, making a sentence about maintaining balance too late in the sequence.
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For inference questions, only the passage counts as evidence. Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions.
3. Main Idea = first sentence + last sentence. Do not get pulled into middle-paragraph details.
4. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
5. Sentence Insertion without signal words: When there is no discourse marker (Furthermore, However, etc.), focus on pronoun references and demonstratives ("this," "these," "such"). Find the only position where they have a clear referent in the preceding sentence.
