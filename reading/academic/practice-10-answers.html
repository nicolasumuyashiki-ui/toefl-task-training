Academic Reading 攻略 TipsAcademic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
この練習からの言い換え例:Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "fundamentally changed how goods were made and how people lived and worked" → Answer: "transformed society through urbanization, new social classes, and eventual improvements in living standards"
Passage: "the enzyme temporarily changes shape to fit around it more tightly" → Answer: "temporarily adjusts its shape to fit more closely around the substrate"
2 Understand passage structure
First sentence → Topic / Main claim
Middle → Details, examples, data
Last sentence → Conclusion / Summary

Main Idea questions can often be solved by reading just the first and last sentences of each paragraph.
Q1: Paragraph 1 introduces the Industrial Revolution. Paragraph 2 describes urbanization and new social classes. Paragraph 3 covers long-term improvements. The thread: how industrialization transformed society = A.
3 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the target word with each option and see which one reads most naturally in the sentence.
Q2: "One of the most [significant] effects of industrialization was the rapid growth of cities" reads naturally and matches the context of massive societal change. "Temporary" contradicts the lasting impact described; "slight" is the opposite.
4 Rhetorical Purpose: ask "what point is the author making?"
When asked why the author mentions something, look at the sentence before the example. That sentence usually states the point the example is meant to illustrate.
罠のパターン:Trap pattern: An option that accurately describes the example itself but misidentifies the author's purpose for including it.

文脈確認:Context check: 第2段落には「the enzyme temporarily changes shape to fit around it more tightly, a process scientists describe as induced fit」とある。これは直接 B に一致。

A は「permanently」と言うが、パッセージは「temporarily」と言う。C は酵素に変化が起こらないと主張するが、描かれた形状変化と矛盾。D は温度を上げるエネルギー放出に言及するが、パッセージにはどこにも記載なし。 The passage describes how the substrate entering the active site causes the enzyme to change shape — this is called "induced fit." The fit is brought about by the substrate's presence = C.

Substituting: "[brought about] fit" reads naturally, meaning the fit is caused by the substrate. A (prevented) is the opposite. B (random) contradicts the specific, substrate-triggered nature of the process. D (inherited) refers to genetic traits, not a chemical interaction.

Look at the four squares in paragraph 3 that indicate where the following sentence could be added:

"Maintaining this precise balance of temperature and pH is essential because the enzyme’s three-dimensional shape depends on weak chemical bonds that are easily disrupted."
正解: BCorrect: B あなたの回答: —Your answer: —
A 文1の後（「…enzyme activity.」の後）After sentence 1 (after "...enzyme activity.")
B 文2の後（「…and at a neutral pH level.」の後） ✓After sentence 2 (after "...and at a neutral pH level.") ✓
C 文3の後（「…a process known as denaturation.」の後）After sentence 3 (after "...a process known as denaturation.")
D 文4の後（「…loses its catalytic function entirely.」の後）After sentence 4 (after "...loses its catalytic function entirely.")
Why B is correct (no discourse marker — content reasoning required): パッセージは基質が活性部位に入ると酵素が形状を変えることを描き、これが「induced fit」と呼ばれる。フィットは基質の存在によって引き起こされる = C。

代入：「[引き起こされた]フィット」自然に読め、フィットが基質によって引き起こされることを意味する。A（防がれる）は逆。B（ランダム）はプロセスの基質誘発的な性質と矛盾。D（遺伝的）は遺伝的形質を指し、化学的相互作用ではない。 The insertion sentence mentions "this precise balance of temperature and pH." The demonstrative "this" requires a clear referent. Sentence 2 is the only sentence that specifies both the precise temperature (37°C) and the precise pH level (neutral). After sentence 2, "this precise balance" has a clear referent = B.

A is incorrect because sentence 1 is a general statement about environmental conditions; no specific temperature or pH values have been stated yet, so "this precise balance" has no referent. C is incorrect because by sentence 3, the paragraph has already moved to what happens when conditions deviate (denaturation); inserting a sentence about maintaining balance after the damage is already described would be illogical. D is incorrect for the same reason — the enzyme has already lost function, making a sentence about maintaining balance too late in the sequence.
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For inference questions, only the passage counts as evidence. Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions.
3. Main Idea = first sentence + last sentence. Do not get pulled into middle-paragraph details.
4. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
5. Sentence Insertion without signal words: When there is no discourse marker (Furthermore, However, etc.), focus on pronoun references and demonstratives ("this," "these," "such"). Find the only position where they have a clear referent in the preceding sentence.
