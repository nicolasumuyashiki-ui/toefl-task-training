Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "shelter inside its hollow thorns and a food source through specialized nectar-producing glands" → Answer: "Housing within the tree's spiny structures and nutrition produced by the plant's own secretions"
Passage: "gaps created by dying host trees allow sunlight to reach the forest floor, promoting new growth" → Answer: "benefit the ecosystem by creating opportunities for new plant growth"
2 Understand passage structure
First sentence → Topic / Main claim
Middle → Details, examples, data
Last sentence → Conclusion / Summary

Main Idea questions can often be solved by reading just the first and last sentences of each paragraph.
Q1: Paragraph 1 introduces jazz's origins in New Orleans. Paragraph 2 describes its spread in the 1920s. Paragraph 3 covers later subgenres. The thread: how jazz originated and developed = A.
3 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the target word with each option and see which one reads most naturally in the sentence.
Q5: "the fig's roots [squeeze tightly] the host tree's trunk" reads naturally and matches the context of roots wrapping around and killing the tree. "Support" is the opposite; "decorate" and "gently touch" do not convey the destructive nature described.
4 Rhetorical Purpose: ask "what point is the author making?"
When asked why the author mentions something, look at the sentence before the example. That sentence usually states the point the example is meant to illustrate.
Trap pattern: An option that accurately describes the example itself but misidentifies the author's purpose for including it.
The Evolution of Jazz Music in America Art & Music
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A How jazz originated and developed through different periods and styles in the United States ✓
B Why Louis Armstrong is considered the greatest jazz musician of all time
C The reasons why jazz became less popular after the 1920s
D A comparison between jazz and classical music traditions
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 describes jazz's origins in late 19th-century New Orleans. Paragraph 2 traces its spread to Chicago and New York in the 1920s. Paragraph 3 covers subsequent subgenres (bebop, cool jazz, fusion). The common thread across all three paragraphs is how jazz originated and evolved = A.

B focuses on only one musician mentioned in paragraph 2 and uses extreme language ("greatest"). C is contradicted by the passage, which describes continued evolution, not decline. D is unsupported — classical music is never mentioned.
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "sophisticated" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A extremely loud
B complex and refined ✓
C traditional and simple
D foreign and unfamiliar
Context check: The passage says musicians introduced "more sophisticated arrangements and solo performances," describing how they "pushed the boundaries of the genre." This context suggests the arrangements became more complex and refined = B.

Substituting: "more [complex and refined] arrangements" reads naturally. A (extremely loud) has no support. C (traditional and simple) is the opposite of pushing boundaries. D (foreign) does not fit the context of artistic development.
Question 3 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie in paragraph 3?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A To argue that bebop was superior to earlier forms of jazz
B To explain why jazz eventually lost its audience
C To identify key figures who helped develop a new subgenre of jazz ✓
D To compare the musical training of bebop and cool jazz musicians
Explanation: The sentence before the names states: "Bebop, pioneered by artists like Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie, emphasized fast tempos and complex harmonies." The purpose of mentioning them is to identify the key figures who pioneered bebop, a new subgenre = C.

A uses the word "superior," which the passage never claims. B is unsupported — the passage does not say jazz lost its audience. D mentions "musical training," which is not discussed.
Symbiosis in Tropical Rainforest Ecosystems Life Science
Question 4 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, what do acacia trees provide to the ants that live in them?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A Protection from predators and access to water sources
B A place to live inside hollow thorns and a food source from nectar glands ✓
C Seeds for reproduction and chemical defenses against disease
D Leaves that the ants use to build underground nests
Explanation: Paragraph 2 states: "The acacia provides the ants with shelter inside its hollow thorns and a food source through specialized nectar-producing glands" = B.

A is partially correct (shelter could imply protection), but the passage does not mention "water sources." C and D describe things not mentioned in the passage. Always match the answer precisely to what the passage states.
Paraphrase: Passage: "shelter inside its hollow thorns" → B: "A place to live inside hollow thorns"
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "constrict" in paragraph 3 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A support
B decorate
C squeeze tightly ✓
D gently touch
Context check: The passage says roots "wrap tightly around the host tree" and then "constrict the host tree's trunk," eventually causing the host to die. The context of wrapping tightly and causing death indicates squeezing = C.

Substituting: "the fig's roots [squeeze tightly] the host tree's trunk and block sunlight" reads naturally. A (support) is the opposite of the destructive context. B (decorate) and D (gently touch) do not match the harmful outcome described.
Question 6 — Inference
What can be inferred from the passage about the death of host trees caused by strangler figs?
Correct: D Your answer: —
A It permanently reduces the total number of species in the rainforest.
B It has no measurable effect on the surrounding ecosystem.
C It is the primary cause of tropical rainforest destruction worldwide.
D Young seedlings on the forest floor near a strangler fig are more likely to survive than those under a dense canopy. ✓
Text-based inference: The passage states: "the gaps created by dying host trees allow sunlight to reach the forest floor, promoting new growth and contributing to the ongoing cycle of renewal within the ecosystem." This directly supports D — the death of host trees can benefit the ecosystem by enabling new growth.

A uses "permanently" which is too extreme and contradicted by the "cycle of renewal." B directly contradicts the passage, which describes clear ecological effects. C uses extreme language ("primary cause") with no support — the passage describes a natural process, not destruction.
Paraphrase: Passage: "promoting new growth and contributing to the ongoing cycle of renewal" → D: "benefit the ecosystem by creating opportunities for new plant growth"
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For inference questions, only the passage counts as evidence. Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions.
3. Main Idea = first sentence + last sentence. Do not get pulled into middle-paragraph details.
4. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
