Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "appearance may unfairly influence outcomes" → Answer: "can lead to unfair judgments based on a single positive trait"
Passage: "sedimentary rocks often contain fossils" → Answer: "can provide evidence of organisms that lived in the past"
2 Understand passage structure
First sentence → Topic / Main claim
Middle → Details, examples, data
Last sentence → Conclusion / Summary

Main Idea questions can often be solved by reading just the first and last sentences.
Q1: Passage 1 opens with how people form rapid opinions upon meeting someone and ends with the importance of understanding these biases. The thread is how first impressions form and why they persist = B.
3 Inference must come from the text only
Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions. Only information stated in the passage counts as evidence.
Most common mistake:
✓ Passage says "sedimentary rocks often contain fossils" → "they can provide evidence of past organisms" (valid inference)
✗ Passage says "sedimentary rocks often contain fossils" → "they form more quickly" (unsupported — the passage says it takes thousands or millions of years)
4 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the target word with each option and see which one reads most naturally in the sentence. The correct meaning must fit both the sentence and the broader paragraph context.
Q5: "existing rocks are subjected to extreme heat" → try "exposed to extreme heat" — reads naturally and matches the meaning. "Resistant to" would create the opposite meaning.
The Psychology of First Impressions Social Science
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A The importance of physical appearance in job interviews
B How first impressions are formed and why they are hard to change ✓
C A comparison of different psychological research methods
D The history of psychology as an academic discipline
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 introduces how people form rapid first impressions. Paragraph 2 discusses the halo effect bias. Paragraph 3 explains why first impressions are hard to change (anchoring). The thread connecting all paragraphs is how first impressions work and their persistence = B.

A is too narrow — job interviews are mentioned only as one example. C and D are not discussed in the passage at all.
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "cognitive" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A emotional
B physical
C related to mental processes ✓
D socially acceptable
Context check: The passage describes the halo effect as "a cognitive bias in which a single positive trait... leads people to assume..." This is about how the mind processes information and makes judgments. "Cognitive" means relating to thinking or mental processes = C.

A (emotional) relates to feelings, not thinking. B (physical) describes the body. D (socially acceptable) has no connection to mental processing.
Question 3 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, why is the halo effect significant?
Correct: D Your answer: —
A It causes people to form impressions more slowly than usual.
B It proves that first impressions are always accurate.
C It helps people make better decisions in professional settings.
D It can lead to biased evaluations based on one favorable characteristic. ✓
Explanation: Paragraph 2 states that the halo effect causes people to "assume that the person also possesses other positive qualities" based on one trait, and that "appearance may unfairly influence outcomes" in job interviews and courtrooms = D.

A contradicts the passage — first impressions happen within seconds. B uses the extreme word "always" and the passage never claims impressions are accurate. C states the opposite of what the passage says — the halo effect leads to unfair outcomes, not better decisions.
Paraphrase: Passage: "appearance may unfairly influence outcomes" → D: "can lead to unfair judgments based on a single positive trait"
The Formation and Classification of Rocks Physical Science
Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention granite and basalt in paragraph 2?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A To illustrate how different cooling speeds produce different igneous rocks ✓
B To argue that igneous rocks are more useful than sedimentary rocks
C To explain why volcanic eruptions are becoming less frequent
D To compare igneous rocks with metamorphic rocks
Explanation: Paragraph 2 discusses how igneous rocks form through cooling. Granite is introduced as an example of slow cooling (large crystals), while basalt is an example of rapid cooling (fine-grained). The two examples serve to illustrate how different cooling speeds produce different types of igneous rock = A.

B makes a comparative claim not found in the passage. C discusses eruption frequency, which is not mentioned. D is wrong because metamorphic rocks are discussed in a separate paragraph, not compared here.
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "subjected" in paragraph 4 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A resistant to
B exposed to ✓
C protected from
D attracted to
Context check: The sentence reads "existing rocks are subjected to extreme heat, pressure, or chemical processes." The next sentence explains that "these conditions cause the minerals... to reorganize." Being "subjected to" something means being forced to experience it, which is equivalent to being "exposed to" it = B.

A (resistant to) means the opposite — able to withstand rather than undergo. C (protected from) also implies avoidance rather than experience. D (attracted to) implies a pulling force that does not fit the context.
Question 6 — Inference
Based on the passage, what can be inferred about sedimentary rocks?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A They form more quickly than igneous or metamorphic rocks.
B They are the hardest and most durable type of rock.
C A geologist studying rock layers with fossils can conclude those layers were likely not formed by volcanic activity. ✓
D They are only found near rivers and oceans.
Text-based inference: Paragraph 3 states that "sedimentary rocks often contain fossils" and that they are "particularly valuable for studying the history of life on Earth." Fossils are preserved remains of organisms that lived in the past. Therefore, sedimentary rocks can provide evidence of past life = C.

A directly contradicts the passage — the process takes "thousands or millions of years." B has no support in the text. D is wrong because the passage says materials are deposited by "wind, water, or ice," not exclusively near rivers and oceans.
Paraphrase: Passage: "often contain fossils... valuable for studying the history of life" → C: "can provide evidence of organisms that lived in the past" — "fossils" is paraphrased as "evidence of organisms"
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For inference questions, only the passage counts as evidence. Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions.
3. Main Idea = first sentence + last sentence. Do not get pulled into middle-paragraph details.
4. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
