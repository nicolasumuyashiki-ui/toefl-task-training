Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases (パラフレーズを見抜く)
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "seeps into the ground, replenishing underground aquifers" → Answer: "soaks into the ground to refill aquifers"
Passage: "grooming may also serve as a strategic tool for gaining social advantages" → Answer: "serves social and strategic purposes beyond basic hygiene"
2 Vocabulary: identify the part of speech first (品詞特定)
Before looking at options, determine if the target word is a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb. Then substitute each option into the sentence to check which one fits both grammatically and logically.
Q2: "it cools and condenses into tiny droplets" — "condenses" is a verb. Substitute: "changes from gas to liquid into tiny droplets" reads naturally. "Disappears completely" and "expands rapidly" contradict the context of forming droplets.
3 Rhetorical Purpose: read the sentence before and after (前後の文を読む)
When asked "Why does the author mention X?", the answer is usually found in the sentence immediately before the mention. The author states a claim, then provides X as an example or evidence.
Q4: The sentence before says "the range of social structures found in primates is remarkably diverse." The mention of marmosets (small groups) and baboons (large hierarchies) illustrates this diversity = A.
4 Use elimination for difficult questions (消去法)
When unsure, eliminate options that contain extreme words (always, never, only, all, none) or information not found in the passage.
Red-flag words to watch for:
✗ "always," "never," "only," "all," "none," "completely," "the most"
✓ Correct answers tend to use moderate language: "some," "often," "may," "tend to"
The Water Cycle and Its Role in Weather Patterns Earth Science
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A How the water cycle functions and influences weather conditions ✓
B Why droughts are becoming more frequent worldwide
C The chemical composition of water vapor in the atmosphere
D A comparison of freshwater and saltwater ecosystems
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 describes evaporation and cloud formation. Paragraph 2 explains precipitation and where the water goes. Paragraph 3 connects the water cycle to weather patterns. The thread connecting all paragraphs is how the water cycle works and affects weather = A.

B is too narrow — droughts are mentioned only briefly in the last sentence. C is never discussed. D is not the topic — the passage focuses on the water cycle, not ecosystem comparison.
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "condenses" in paragraph 1 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A disappears completely
B changes from gas to liquid ✓
C expands rapidly
D breaks into smaller parts
Context check: The passage says vapor "cools and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds." The vapor (gas) is becoming droplets (liquid), so "condenses" means changes from gas to liquid = B.

A is the opposite — the water does not disappear; it becomes droplets. C is the opposite — condensation involves contraction, not expansion. D does not fit the context of forming droplets from vapor.
Question 3 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, what happens to some precipitation after it falls on land?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A It is immediately absorbed by plants and animals.
B It evaporates back into space beyond the atmosphere.
C It flows over the surface or soaks into the ground to refill aquifers. ✓
D It remains frozen on the land surface permanently.
Explanation: Paragraph 2 states: "Some of this water flows over the land as surface runoff" and "The rest seeps into the ground, replenishing underground aquifers" = C.

A mentions plants and animals, which are not discussed. B says "into space," but the passage only mentions evaporation into the atmosphere, not beyond it. D says "permanently," which is an extreme qualifier not supported by the passage.
Paraphrase: Passage: "flows over the land as surface runoff" + "seeps into the ground, replenishing underground aquifers" → C: "flows over the surface or soaks into the ground to refill aquifers"
The Evolution of Social Behavior in Primates Life Science
Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention marmosets and baboons in paragraph 1?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A To illustrate the diversity of social structures among primates ✓
B To argue that baboons are more intelligent than marmosets
C To explain why marmosets are endangered in the wild
D To compare the diets of different primate species
Explanation: Paragraph 1 states: "the range of social structures found in primates is remarkably diverse" and then mentions marmosets (small family groups) and baboons (large hierarchical communities) as contrasting examples of this diversity = A.

B introduces intelligence, which is not the point of paragraph 1. C mentions endangerment, which is never discussed. D mentions diets, which are not the topic here.
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "cognitive" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A physical
B emotional
C related to mental processes and thinking ✓
D relating to instinctive behavior
Context check: The passage says "recognizing allies and rivals, and navigating complex social hierarchies all require significant cognitive resources" and links this to "the neocortex, the part of the brain associated with higher-order thinking." The context clearly associates "cognitive" with brain-based thinking processes = C.

A (physical) relates to the body, not the brain. B (emotional) is too narrow. D (instinctive) implies automatic behavior, but the passage describes deliberate, complex mental processes.
Question 6 — Inference
Based on the passage, what can be inferred about grooming behavior in primates?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A It is performed equally among all members of a group regardless of rank.
B It serves social and strategic purposes beyond basic hygiene. ✓
C It occurs only between primates that are closely related by blood.
D It has been replaced by vocal communication in most modern primates.
Text-based inference: The passage states grooming "acts as a form of social bonding that strengthens alliances and reduces tension" and "may also serve as a strategic tool for gaining social advantages." This goes well beyond simple hygiene = B.

A directly contradicts the passage — primates spend more time grooming higher-ranked individuals, not equally. C uses "only" (extreme word) and blood relation is never mentioned. D introduces "vocal communication" which is not discussed.
Paraphrase: Passage: "Rather than serving only a hygienic function, grooming acts as a form of social bonding" + "a strategic tool" → B: "serves social and strategic purposes beyond basic hygiene"
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For vocabulary questions, identify the part of speech first, then substitute each option. Check which one reads most naturally in context.
3. For rhetorical purpose, read the sentence before the mention. The claim comes first, then the example.
4. Eliminate options with extreme words (always, never, only, all). TOEFL correct answers use moderate language.
