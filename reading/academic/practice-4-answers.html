Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "carried not only goods but also sacred texts, philosophies, and cultural practices" → Answer: "carried goods as well as religious texts and cultural practices"
Passage: "develop crop varieties that are more resilient to environmental stress" → Answer: "crops that can better withstand environmental challenges"
2 Rhetorical Purpose: ask "why," not "what"
Purpose questions ask why the author mentions something, not what it means. Look at the sentence before the mentioned detail — it usually contains the claim the example is meant to support.
Q4: Before mentioning cacti and rainforest plants, paragraph 3 states that "different species have developed remarkable adaptations." The examples illustrate this claim = D.
3 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the highlighted word with each answer choice and see which one reads most naturally and preserves the original meaning.
Q2: "One of the most deep and far-reaching effects of Silk Road contact..." reads naturally and matches the context of major religious and cultural transmission = C.
4 Inference: only the passage counts as evidence
Do not mix in your own knowledge. The answer must be logically supported by specific information in the passage.
Watch for extreme language: Words like "only," "always," "all," and "never" in answer choices are often signs of an incorrect option. The passage rarely makes such absolute claims.
The Silk Road and Cultural Exchange History
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A How the Silk Road facilitated the exchange of culture, religion, and knowledge between civilizations ✓
B The types of luxury goods that were traded along the Silk Road
C The military conflicts that arose from competition over Silk Road trade routes
D A comparison of trade practices in ancient China and the Roman Empire
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 introduces the Silk Road and states its significance "extended far beyond commerce" into ideas, religions, and technologies. Paragraph 2 discusses the spread of religious beliefs. Paragraph 3 covers the exchange of scientific and technological knowledge. The unifying theme is cultural and intellectual exchange = A.

B is too narrow — goods are mentioned only briefly in the first paragraph. C (military conflicts) is never discussed. D mentions a comparison that does not exist in the passage.
Paraphrase: Passage: "spread of ideas, religions, technologies, and artistic styles across diverse civilizations" → A: "exchange of culture, religion, and knowledge between civilizations"
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "profound" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A unexpected
B temporary
C deep and far-reaching ✓
D well-documented
Context check: The sentence reads "One of the most profound effects of Silk Road contact was the transmission of religious beliefs," followed by examples of Buddhism spreading across entire continents. These were clearly deep and far-reaching effects = C. Substituting: "One of the most deep and far-reaching effects" reads naturally.

A (unexpected) — the passage does not suggest the effects were surprising. B (temporary) is the opposite; the religious changes were lasting. D (well-documented) describes the historical record, not the nature of the effects.
Question 3 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, what role did merchants and monks play along the Silk Road?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A They established the first universities in Central Asia.
B They carried goods as well as religious texts and cultural practices to other societies. ✓
C They prevented the spread of disease between eastern and western regions.
D They competed with diplomats for control of key trading posts.
Explanation: Paragraph 2 states: "Merchants, monks, and diplomats carried not only goods but also sacred texts, philosophies, and cultural practices that transformed the societies they reached." This directly supports B.

A (universities) is never mentioned. C (disease prevention) is not discussed. D describes competition between merchants and diplomats, but the passage lists them together as cooperating carriers, not competitors.
Paraphrase: Passage: "carried not only goods but also sacred texts, philosophies, and cultural practices" → B: "carried goods as well as religious texts and cultural practices" — "sacred texts" is paraphrased as "religious texts"
Photosynthesis and Plant Adaptation Life Science
Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention cacti and tropical rainforest plants in paragraph 3?
Correct: D Your answer: —
A To show that photosynthesis occurs only in extreme environments
B To explain why chlorophyll reflects green light
C To argue that desert plants are more efficient at photosynthesis than rainforest plants
D To illustrate how different plants have adapted their photosynthetic strategies to suit their environments ✓
Explanation: Paragraph 3 opens by stating that "different species have developed remarkable adaptations to survive in challenging environments." Cacti (CAM photosynthesis in deserts) and rainforest plants (broad leaves for light capture) are then presented as specific examples of this claim = D.

A uses the extreme word "only" — the passage does not say photosynthesis occurs only in extreme environments. B refers to content from paragraph 2, not paragraph 3. C makes a comparison of efficiency that the passage never makes.
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "remarkable" in paragraph 3 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A ordinary
B risky
C impressive and worthy of attention ✓
D recently discovered
Context check: The sentence reads "different species have developed remarkable adaptations to survive in challenging environments." The word describes adaptations that are notable and impressive, such as CAM photosynthesis in cacti. Substituting: "impressive and worthy of attention adaptations" fits naturally = C.

A (ordinary) is the opposite of "remarkable." B (risky) does not fit — the adaptations are beneficial, not dangerous. D (recently discovered) describes the timing of discovery, not the quality of the adaptations.
Question 6 — Sentence Insertion
Look at the four squares in paragraph 4 that indicate where the following sentence could be added:

"Furthermore, these improved crops would be especially valuable in tropical regions, where rising temperatures already threaten staple food production."
Correct: B Your answer: —
A After sentence 1 (after "...in the context of climate change.")
B After sentence 2 (after "...more resilient to environmental stress.") ✓
C After sentence 3 (after "...agriculture and food security.")
Discourse marker clue: The word "Furthermore" signals that the sentence adds information to a closely related preceding idea. Sentence 2 discusses developing "crop varieties that are more resilient to environmental stress," and the insertion sentence elaborates on where those crops would be most useful. "These improved crops" directly refers back to the resilient crop varieties mentioned in sentence 2 = B.

A is incorrect because sentence 1 introduces general interest in climate change but does not mention crops, so "these improved crops" would have no clear referent. C is incorrect because sentence 3 is a concluding statement about the broader implications of photosynthesis research; inserting a specific detail about tropical regions after the conclusion would disrupt the paragraph's logical flow.
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For Rhetorical Purpose questions, look at the sentence before the detail. The claim the example supports is usually right before it.
3. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
4. Beware of extreme words (only, always, never, all). These are common in incorrect answer choices.
