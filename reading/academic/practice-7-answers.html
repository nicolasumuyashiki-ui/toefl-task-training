Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "prevent any single individual from gaining too much control" → Answer: "prevent one person from holding too much power"
Passage: "mount a much faster and stronger response" → Answer: "respond more quickly"
2 Understand passage structure
First sentence → Topic / Main claim
Middle → Details, examples, data
Last sentence → Conclusion / Summary

Main Idea questions can often be solved by reading just the first and last sentences.
Q1: Passage 1 opens with the Republic being "established around 509 BCE" and ends with "the end of a political experiment that had lasted nearly five hundred years." This arc — establishment to collapse — gives the main idea = A.
3 Rhetorical purpose: ask "why did the author include this?"
Purpose questions ask about the function of a detail, not the detail itself. Look at the sentence before the mentioned detail — it usually states the point the example supports.
Q4: Before mentioning neutrophils and macrophages, the passage says "general-purpose immune cells that respond quickly to any foreign substance." The examples illustrate cells involved in the body's immediate defense = A.
4 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the highlighted word with each answer choice and see which one reads most naturally in context. The correct answer should preserve the original meaning of the sentence.
Common trap: An answer that matches the word's dictionary definition but does not fit the specific context of the passage.
The Rise and Fall of the Roman Republic History
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A How the Roman Republic was established, expanded, and eventually collapsed ✓
B The military strategies that made Rome the strongest empire in history
C A detailed biography of Julius Caesar and his rise to power
D The differences between Roman and Greek systems of government
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 describes the Republic's establishment. Paragraph 2 covers its expansion and growing problems. Paragraph 3 describes its collapse and transformation into an Empire. The entire passage traces this arc = A.

B focuses only on military aspects and calls Rome an "empire" throughout, missing the Republic focus. C mentions Caesar, but he is only one detail in paragraph 3, not the main topic. D mentions Greece, which is never discussed in the passage.
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "tensions" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A celebrations
B conflicts and disagreements ✓
C improvements
D traditions
Context check: The passage says expansion "created serious tensions within Roman society" and then describes generals building personal armies and political rivalries growing "increasingly violent." This context clearly indicates conflicts and disagreements = B.

A (celebrations) is the opposite of the context. C (improvements) contradicts the negative tone. D (traditions) has no support — the passage describes new problems, not old customs.
Question 3 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, what was the purpose of having two consuls lead the Republic?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A To allow citizens to vote directly on laws
B To ensure that military campaigns were always successful
C To prevent one person from holding too much power ✓
D To represent the interests of both wealthy and poor citizens equally
Explanation: Paragraph 1 states: "This structure was designed to prevent any single individual from gaining too much control over the state" = C.

A describes direct democracy, which is not mentioned. B is unsupported — military success is not given as the reason. D mentions representing both rich and poor, but the passage says the Senate consisted of "wealthy and influential citizens," suggesting no equal representation.
Paraphrase: Passage: "prevent any single individual from gaining too much control" → C: "prevent one person from holding too much power"
How the Human Immune System Fights Disease Life Science
Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention neutrophils and macrophages in paragraph 2?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A To give examples of cells involved in the body's immediate defense ✓
B To explain how vaccines stimulate the immune system
C To contrast the functions of B lymphocytes and T lymphocytes
D To argue that innate immunity is more important than adaptive immunity
Explanation: Paragraph 2 discusses innate immunity as "the body's first line of defense" and mentions "general-purpose immune cells that respond quickly." Neutrophils and macrophages are given as examples of these cells = A.

B discusses vaccines, which appear in paragraph 4, not paragraph 2. C mentions B and T lymphocytes, which are in paragraph 3. D makes a comparison claim that the passage never supports — no ranking of importance is given.
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "mobilize" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A disappear
B divide into smaller parts
C become organized and ready for action ✓
D slowly weaken over time
Context check: The sentence reads "these cells immediately mobilize to contain and destroy the threat." The word describes a rapid, organized response to danger. Substituting: "these cells immediately become organized and ready for action to contain and destroy the threat" reads naturally = C.

A (disappear) is the opposite of responding. B (divide) describes cell division, not a response to threats. D (slowly weaken) contradicts "immediately" and the active context.
Question 6 — Inference
Based on the passage, what can be inferred about a person who has recovered from a particular viral infection?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A They will never be exposed to that virus again.
B Receiving a vaccine against a disease they have already recovered from would provide limited additional benefit. ✓
C Their innate immune system will become permanently stronger.
D They no longer need physical barriers such as skin to protect them.
Text-based inference: Paragraph 4 states that memory cells allow the immune system to "mount a much faster and stronger response" when the same pathogen returns, "often eliminating the threat before any symptoms appear." A recovered person would have these memory cells = B.

A is wrong because having immunity does not prevent exposure — the passage says the immune system responds faster, not that exposure is avoided. C confuses innate and adaptive immunity — memory cells are part of adaptive immunity, not innate. D is absurd — physical barriers remain necessary regardless of past infections.
Paraphrase: Passage: "mount a much faster and stronger response" → B: "respond more quickly" — simplified but accurate paraphrase
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For inference questions, only the passage counts as evidence. Do not mix in your own knowledge or opinions.
3. Main Idea = first sentence + last sentence. Do not get pulled into middle-paragraph details.
4. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
