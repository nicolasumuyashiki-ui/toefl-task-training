Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "a substance that inhibited bacterial growth" → Answer: "prevented or slowed down" bacterial growth
Passage: "providing strong evidence that not everything revolved around the Earth" → Answer: "provided evidence against the Earth-centered model"
2 Rhetorical Purpose: ask "why did the author include this?"
Purpose questions ask about the function of a detail, not its content. Ask yourself: "What point is the author trying to make by mentioning this?" The answer usually connects the detail back to the paragraph's main claim.
Q4: Galileo's discovery of Jupiter's moons is mentioned right after the topic sentence about telescopes transforming astronomy. The discovery serves as evidence that telescopes revealed facts challenging the geocentric model = A.
3 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the highlighted word with each answer choice. The correct answer must make sense in the specific sentence, not just as a dictionary definition.
Common mistake: Choosing a meaning that is technically correct for the word but does not fit the context. Always re-read the sentence with your chosen answer plugged in.
4 Inference: each advance = new capability
When a passage describes a series of developments, look for the pattern. If each new technology is described as revealing something new, you can infer that progress means expanded capability.
Q6: The passage describes optical telescopes revealing moons, reflecting telescopes observing fainter objects, radio telescopes detecting invisible radiation, and space telescopes imaging distant galaxies. The pattern: each advance revealed previously undetectable phenomena = C.
The Discovery of Penicillin Life Science
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A How penicillin was discovered and eventually developed into a widely used medicine ✓
B Why Alexander Fleming is considered the greatest scientist of the twentieth century
C The dangers of bacterial infections in modern hospitals
D The differences between molds and bacteria in laboratory research
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 sets up the problem of bacterial infections before penicillin. Paragraph 2 describes Fleming's 1928 discovery. Paragraph 3 explains how Florey and Chain later turned penicillin into a practical drug. The passage traces the full arc from discovery to medical application = A.

B overstates Fleming's importance; the passage does not rank him among other scientists. C discusses hospitals, which the passage does not focus on. D is a minor laboratory detail, not the central topic.
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "inhibited" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A encouraged
B prevented or slowed down ✓
C observed carefully
D identified and named
Context check: The previous sentence states that "the area around the mold was completely clear of growth." So the mold was releasing a substance that stopped the bacteria from growing — meaning it prevented or slowed down bacterial growth = B.

A (encouraged) is the opposite of the correct meaning. C (observed) confuses Fleming's action with the mold's effect. D (identified) describes naming, not biological activity.
Question 3 — Inference
What can be inferred from paragraph 3 about the contribution of Howard Florey and Ernst Chain?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A They were the first scientists to observe that mold could kill bacteria.
B They discovered a completely new substance that replaced penicillin.
C Their work made it possible to use penicillin as a practical treatment for patients. ✓
D They proved that Fleming's original findings about penicillin were incorrect.
Inference logic: Paragraph 3 says penicillin remained a "laboratory curiosity" until Florey and Chain "developed techniques to isolate and manufacture penicillin in quantities large enough for medical use," and that mass production then "reduced deaths from infected wounds." You can infer that their contribution was what allowed penicillin to become a usable medical treatment = C.

A is incorrect because Fleming, not Florey and Chain, first observed the effect. B is wrong because they worked on the same substance (penicillin), not a replacement. D contradicts the passage — their work confirmed and extended Fleming's findings rather than disproving them.
Note: Inference questions never require outside knowledge. The correct answer must be a logical conclusion supported directly by specific sentences in the passage.
How Telescopes Changed Our Understanding of the Universe Physical Science
Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention Galileo's discovery of four moons orbiting Jupiter?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A To show how telescopes provided evidence against the Earth-centered model of the universe ✓
B To argue that Jupiter is more important than other planets in the solar system
C To explain why Galileo invented the telescope
D To compare ancient and modern methods of counting moons
Explanation: Paragraph 2 opens by identifying Galileo as an early telescope user. The discovery of Jupiter's moons is immediately followed by the statement that this provided "strong evidence that not everything in the sky revolved around the Earth," which "directly challenged the geocentric model." The purpose is to illustrate how telescopes challenged established beliefs = A.

B misinterprets the example; Jupiter's importance is not the point. C is factually wrong; the passage says Galileo was "among the first to use" a telescope, not that he invented it. D is not discussed.
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "dominated" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A confused
B controlled or had the greatest influence over ✓
C briefly appeared in
D was rejected by
Context check: The sentence reads "the geocentric model that had dominated European thought for over a thousand years." Substituting: "the geocentric model that had controlled or had the greatest influence over European thought for over a thousand years" = B. This reads naturally and captures the idea that the geocentric view was the prevailing belief.

A (confused) does not fit. C ("briefly appeared") contradicts "over a thousand years." D (rejected) is the opposite — the passage says it was dominant, not rejected.
Question 6 — Sentence Insertion
Look at the four squares in paragraph 2 that indicate where the following sentence could be added:

"Furthermore, his detailed drawings of these moons showed that they changed position from night to night, confirming that they were indeed in orbit."
Correct: B Your answer: —
A After sentence 1 (after "...systematic astronomical observation.")
B After sentence 2 (after "...not everything in the sky revolved around the Earth.") ✓
C After sentence 3 (after "...European thought for over a thousand years.")
D After sentence 4 (after "...rather than perfect, unchanging spheres.")
Discourse marker clue: "Furthermore" signals additional supporting evidence for the preceding idea. Sentence 2 states that Galileo discovered four moons orbiting Jupiter. The insertion sentence adds further detail about those same moons — "his detailed drawings of these moons" directly refers to the moons introduced in sentence 2 = B.

A is incorrect because sentence 1 is a general introduction about Galileo using a telescope; no moons have been mentioned yet, so "these moons" has no referent. C is incorrect because sentence 3 discusses the impact on the geocentric model, shifting topic away from the moons. D is incorrect because sentence 4 moves on to Venus and the Moon's surface, making a return to Jupiter's moons disruptive to the flow.
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. For rhetorical purpose questions, connect the detail to the paragraph's main claim. The detail serves as evidence or illustration.
3. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and re-read. The correct answer must fit the specific sentence naturally.
4. For inference questions, look for patterns across the passage. If the passage describes a series of advances, the inference is about the overall trend.
5. For Sentence Insertion, use discourse markers as clues. Words like "Furthermore" and "As a result" signal how the sentence connects to its neighbors. Also check pronoun references like "these moons" — they must have a clear referent before the insertion point.
