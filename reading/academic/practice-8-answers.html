Academic Reading Strategy Tips
1 Correct answers are always paraphrases
The correct answer restates the passage in different words. Be suspicious of options that copy exact words from the passage — they are often traps.
Paraphrase examples from this practice:
Passage: "heavily influenced by cognitive biases" → Answer: "How cognitive biases affect the way people make decisions"
Passage: "providing a unified framework that explains" → Answer: "connects many different geological phenomena under one explanation"
2 Rhetorical Purpose: ask "Why did the author include this?"
When you see a specific example in the passage, ask yourself: What general point was the author trying to support? The answer to a purpose question is never about the example itself — it is about the larger claim.
Q4: The Himalayas are mentioned right after the sentence about plate collision causing mountain formation. The example illustrates this process = D.
3 Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check
Replace the highlighted word with each option and read the sentence. The option that fits most naturally is the answer.
Common trap: An option that is a valid dictionary definition of the word but does not fit the specific context of the passage.
4 Sentence Insertion: follow the referents
When there are no discourse markers (like "Furthermore" or "In contrast"), look for pronoun references and vocabulary echoes that connect the new sentence to a specific preceding sentence.
Q6 example: "the range" and "the two plates" in the insertion sentence can only refer to the Himalayas and the Indian/Eurasian Plates mentioned in sentence 3. This tells you the sentence must go at position C.
Common mistake: Choosing a position where the sentence "sounds okay" without checking whether the pronoun references and vocabulary actually match. Always ask: What does "the range" / "these findings" / "such behavior" refer to? The referent must appear before the insertion point.
The Psychology of Decision Making Social Science
Question 1 — Main Idea
What is the passage mainly about?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A The history of psychological research on rational thinking
B How cognitive biases affect the way people make decisions ✓
C Why people should avoid making important decisions quickly
D A comparison of decision-making strategies used by professionals
Structure approach: Paragraph 1 introduces cognitive biases as systematic patterns that affect decision making. Paragraph 2 illustrates the anchoring effect. Paragraph 3 describes the availability heuristic and concludes that understanding biases leads to better decisions. The thread connecting all paragraphs is how biases affect decisions = B.

A is wrong because the passage does not trace the history of psychological research. C is unsupported — the passage does not advise against quick decisions. D is wrong because the passage discusses cognitive biases in general, not professional strategies.
Paraphrase: Passage says "heavily influenced by cognitive biases" and "systematic patterns of thinking" → B says "How cognitive biases affect the way people make decisions"
Question 2 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "arbitrary" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: C Your answer: —
A carefully calculated
B scientifically proven
C random and without reason ✓
D widely recognized
Context check: The passage says the anchor influences estimates "even when it is completely arbitrary." The next sentence confirms this with an example where the number was "randomly generated" from a wheel spin. This means "arbitrary" = random, without logical basis = C.

A (carefully calculated) is the opposite of the meaning. B (scientifically proven) contradicts the idea that the number was random. D (widely recognized) does not fit the context of a random number.
Question 3 — Factual Detail
According to the passage, why do many people believe that flying is more dangerous than driving?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A Because plane crashes receive a lot of media attention and are easily remembered ✓
B Because driving statistics are not available to the general public
C Because psychologists have confirmed that air travel carries higher risks
D Because most people have had a negative personal experience with flying
Explanation: Paragraph 3 explains the availability heuristic: people overestimate the likelihood of events that are "easily recalled from memory." It then states that "dramatic events such as plane crashes receive extensive media coverage," causing the false belief = A.

B is not mentioned — the passage does not discuss public access to statistics. C directly contradicts the passage, which says "statistics show the opposite." D is unsupported — personal experiences with flying are not discussed.
Paraphrase: Passage: "receive extensive media coverage" and "easily recalled from memory" → A: "receive a lot of media attention and are easily remembered"
Plate Tectonics and Mountain Formation Physical Science
Question 4 — Rhetorical Purpose
Why does the author mention the Himalayas in paragraph 2?
Correct: D Your answer: —
A To explain why the Indian Plate is the fastest-moving plate on Earth
B To compare the Himalayas with volcanic mountains like Mount Fuji
C To argue that mountain formation has slowed down over time
D To provide a specific example of mountains formed by plate collision ✓
Explanation: Paragraph 2 opens with a general claim: "When two tectonic plates collide... creating mountain ranges." It then introduces the Himalayas with "for instance," clearly signaling that this is an example of the collision process = D.

A is wrong — the passage never discusses which plate is fastest. B is wrong because the comparison with Mount Fuji occurs in paragraph 3, not paragraph 2. C directly contradicts the passage, which says the Himalayas "are still rising."
Question 5 — Vocabulary in Context
The word "prominent" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
Correct: B Your answer: —
A ancient and eroded
B well-known and important ✓
C difficult to access
D recently discovered
Context check: The passage says orogeny "is responsible for the formation of some of the world's most prominent mountain chains." The Himalayas are then given as an example. "Prominent" here means notable, well-known, and significant = B. Substituting: "most well-known and important mountain chains" reads naturally.

A (ancient and eroded) describes age, not fame or importance. C (difficult to access) is unrelated to the context. D (recently discovered) contradicts the fact that these are well-established mountain ranges.
Question 6 — Sentence Insertion
Where would the following sentence best fit in the passage?
"Precise satellite measurements of the range have confirmed that the two plates continue to move toward each other at a measurable rate."
Correct: C Your answer: —
A After sentence 1 (about plates colliding and creating mountain ranges)
B After sentence 2 (about orogeny and prominent mountain chains)
C After sentence 3 (about the Himalayas and the Indian/Eurasian Plate collision) ✓
D After sentence 4 (about the Himalayas rising 5 mm per year)
Why C is correct: The insertion sentence mentions "the range" and "the two plates." These references only make sense after the sentence that introduces the Himalayas (a specific range) and names the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate (two specific plates). That sentence ends at position C.

Why not A or B? At these positions, no specific mountain range or named pair of plates has been mentioned yet, so "the range" and "the two plates" would lack clear referents.
Why not D? Position D comes after a sentence that already states the Himalayas are "still rising by about five millimeters per year," which already confirms ongoing movement. Placing the satellite-confirmation sentence after that would be redundant and disrupt the logical flow.
Insertion strategy (no discourse markers): When the insertion sentence lacks signal words like "Furthermore" or "In contrast," focus on pronoun references ("the range," "the two plates") and vocabulary echoes that tie back to a specific preceding sentence. Only one position provides the necessary referents.
Key Takeaways
1. Correct answers are always paraphrases of the passage. Options using the same words as the passage are often traps.
2. Rhetorical Purpose = why, not what. The answer explains the author's purpose for including an example, not the example itself.
3. Vocabulary in Context: substitute and check. Replace the word with each option and see which one reads most naturally.
4. Sentence Insertion without signal words: Track pronoun references and vocabulary echoes to find the only position where they have clear referents.
