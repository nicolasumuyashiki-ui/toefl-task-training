Read in Daily Life Strategy Tips
1 News articles: separate facts from background
News articles open with the core event ("who did what, when, where"). Later paragraphs add reasons, quotes, and future plans. For factual questions, locate the specific paragraph that deals with that sub-topic rather than re-reading the whole article.
Q2: The funding question is answered only in paragraph 3: "Funding for the saplings came from a grant... tools and refreshments were donated by local businesses." = C.
2 Inference: check the future-tense sentences
When an article ends with a sentence about "plans to," "will hold," or "goal of," that line is often the basis for inference questions. The writer is telling you what comes next — use that to draw conclusions about the organization or the event.
Q3: "Green Roots plans to hold a second planting session in October, with a goal of reaching 1,500 trees." This directly supports "The group intends to continue its planting work" = B.
3 Social media posts: read the informal tone carefully
Social posts are short and casual, but every phrase carries information. "Honestly, my Portuguese is still rough, but the staff keep switching to English to help me out" shows both a difficulty and a positive response. For inference, ask: "What attitude or situation does the writer's word choice reveal?"
Q5: The coworkers "switching to English to help me out" signals they are being flexible and supportive about the language gap = A.
Passage 1 News Article
Question 1 — Factual Detail
According to the article, why were red maple, river birch, and swamp oak chosen for the planting?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A They grow faster than most other native tree species.
B They do well in damp ground and bring in pollinating insects. ✓
C They produce wood that can be sold to local businesses later.
D They were the only saplings that the state grant covered.
Explanation: The article says the species were chosen because "they thrive in wet soil and attract pollinators." "Wet soil" paraphrases to "damp ground," and "attract pollinators" paraphrases to "bring in pollinating insects" = B.

A (grow faster) and C (wood sales) are not mentioned. D misreads the grant — the grant funded the saplings, but the article gives no suggestion that only these three species were eligible.
Question 2 — Factual Detail
How was the event paid for?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A Volunteers paid small fees to participate in the planting.
B Maplewood town council covered the full cost of supplies.
C A state grant bought the saplings while local shops gave tools and food. ✓
D Green Roots Maplewood sold T-shirts to raise the money needed.
Explanation: Paragraph 3 explains: "Funding for the saplings came from a grant awarded by the state environment office... tools and refreshments were donated by local businesses." This gives two funding sources that exactly match C.

A (volunteer fees), B (town council), and D (T-shirt sales) are all plausible but have no basis in the text.
Question 3 — Inference
What can be inferred about Green Roots Maplewood?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A The group was founded shortly before this particular event.
B The group intends to continue its planting work beyond this event. ✓
C The group mainly focuses on bird-watching rather than planting trees.
D The group will shift its attention to cleaning up the river next year.
Explanation: The final sentence reads: "Green Roots plans to hold a second planting session in October, with a goal of reaching 1,500 trees by next spring." This clearly implies ongoing, future planting activity = B.

A is contradicted by the phrase "largest community tree-planting event to date," which implies a track record. C misreads the birds/insects line — those are beneficiaries, not the group's focus. D invents a river-cleanup project that is never mentioned.
Passage 2 Social Media Post
Question 4 — Factual Detail
According to the post, what does Mia do in the afternoons?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A She attends Portuguese language classes at her university.
B She works as an intern at a design studio in the city center. ✓
C She travels by train to other cities around Portugal.
D She teaches English lessons to local high school students.
Explanation: The post says: "Classes finish at noon, so afternoons are free for the internship at a small design studio downtown." "Downtown" paraphrases to "city center" = B.

A reverses the timing — classes are in the morning, not afternoon. C refers to the upcoming weekend trip to Porto, not her weekday afternoons. D (teaching English) is not mentioned anywhere in the post.
Question 5 — Inference
What can be inferred about Mia's experience at the design studio?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A Her coworkers are being accommodating about the language barrier. ✓
B She has decided to stop using Portuguese in the office completely.
C She is being paid a higher salary than other summer interns.
D She has been asked to lead a major project on her own soon.
Explanation: Mia writes, "my Portuguese is still rough, but the staff keep switching to English to help me out." The coworkers changing languages on her behalf shows they are being flexible and supportive = A.

B overstates the situation — the staff switch for her, but she never says she has stopped using Portuguese. C (salary) and D (leading a project) are not supported anywhere in the post.
