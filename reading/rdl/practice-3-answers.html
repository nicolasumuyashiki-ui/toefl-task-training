Read in Daily Life Strategy Tips
1 Social media posts: read the caption, not just the image
Social media posts pack a lot of information into a short text. The main purpose is usually in the first 1-2 sentences, while specific instructions (what to bring, who to contact) appear later. Hashtags and emoji are decorative — focus on the body paragraphs.
Q1: "Our free Biology 201 study circle is back this week!" plus the meeting time signals an invitation to a recurring meeting = B.
2 Schedules: match the event to the time column
For schedule passages, your finger should move horizontally: find the row, check the time, then read the event. For EXCEPT questions, use the intro or footnotes to catch items that cost extra or require separate tickets.
Trap: "Included with ID" options often hide one paid event (e.g., meal tickets sold separately). Always re-read intro and notes.
3 Inference: find the single supporting sentence
The correct inference answer is logically supported by one specific sentence. If you can't point to a sentence that supports it, it's probably a distractor.
Q6: The schedule note says "the Main Stage events will be moved to the Student Union Ballroom" in case of heavy rain. That directly supports "moved to an indoor space" = C.
Passage 1 Social Media Post
Question 1 — Main Purpose
What is the main purpose of this post?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A To recruit new members for a new biology club
B To invite students to a weekly biology study meeting ✓
C To announce the date of the upcoming biology midterm
D To request volunteers to tutor first-year biology students
Explanation: The post says "our free Biology 201 study circle is back this week" and gives the meeting time and room. The main purpose is to invite students to the weekly study meeting = B.

A is wrong because this is an existing, recurring study circle, not a new club. C is wrong because the post only mentions that midterms are "around the corner" without announcing a date. D is wrong because it asks for attendees, not volunteer tutors.
Question 2 — Factual Information
According to the post, what should participants do if they have food allergies?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A Mention it in the comments section of the post ✓
B Send a direct message to the organizer beforehand
C Bring their own snacks to share with other members
D Skip the snack portion and arrive after the break
Explanation: The post says: "please let us know in the comments if you have allergies so we can plan ahead" = A.

B is wrong because the post asks for comments, not DMs. C is wrong because snacks are provided by the organizers. D is not supported — there is no mention of skipping any portion.
Question 3 — Inference
What can be inferred about students who have never attended before?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A They must register in advance through a website
B They are encouraged to join without prior registration ✓
C They are charged a small fee for their first session
D They should attend only after finishing the textbook
Explanation: The post says "No sign-up needed — just show up. First-timers always welcome!" This supports that newcomers can join freely without registration = B.

A directly contradicts "no sign-up needed." C contradicts the post calling the circle "free." D has no basis in the text.

Question 5 — NOT/EXCEPT

Correct: A Your answer: —

B the opening ceremony on the Main Stage

D the outdoor movie screening in the evening

B — opening ceremony ✓ included (no extra ticket mentioned)

D — movie screening ✓ included

Question 6 — Inference
What can be inferred about the festival if heavy rain occurs?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A All scheduled events will be canceled for the day
B Only the art exhibition will continue as planned
C Main Stage events will be moved to an indoor space ✓
D The festival will be postponed to the following Saturday
Explanation: The schedule note says: "In case of heavy rain, the Main Stage events will be moved to the Student Union Ballroom." The Student Union Ballroom is an indoor venue, so we can infer Main Stage events will be held indoors = C.

A is too extreme — events are relocated, not canceled. B is unsupported; the note focuses on the Main Stage. D has no textual basis.
