Read in Daily Life Strategy Tips
1 Identify the purpose from opening keywords
The first 1-2 sentences reveal the purpose of the text. Learn the patterns:

Notification: We are pleased to inform... / We would like to inform you...
Reminder: This is a reminder that... / ...is pleased to remind you...
Announcement: Beginning [date]... / Effective [date]...
Q1: The email opens with "is pleased to remind you that the application deadline...is Friday, December 6" and then provides program details, requirements, and an information session. This is a reminder about an upcoming deadline = B.
2 For detail questions, locate the key phrase first
Steps:
1. Identify the key phrase in the question (e.g., "requirement for applying")
2. Scan the passage for that exact phrase or a synonym
3. Read the sentence containing it carefully
4. Match it to an answer choice
Q2: The question asks about requirements. Scanning for "must" leads to: "you must have completed at least 30 credit hours and have a cumulative GPA of 2.75 or higher" = C.
3 Inference questions: stick to the text
For inference questions, the answer must be logically supported by information in the passage. Do not mix in your own knowledge or assumptions.
Common trap: An option that sounds reasonable based on real-world knowledge but is NOT supported by the passage text.
Passage 1 Email
Question 1 — Main Purpose
What is the main purpose of this email?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A To announce that the Study Abroad Program has been canceled for next semester
B To remind students about the upcoming application deadline and provide program details ✓
C To notify students that they have been accepted into the exchange program
D To request that faculty members write recommendation letters for students
Explanation: The email reminds students that the application deadline is December 6, explains the eligibility requirements, lists the application materials, and mentions an information session. The overall purpose is to remind students about the deadline and provide details = B.

A is wrong because the program is not canceled. C is wrong because this is about applying, not acceptance. D is directed at students, not faculty.
Question 2 — Factual Detail
According to the email, what is one requirement for applying to the Study Abroad Program?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A Students must have studied a foreign language for at least two years
B Students must attend the information session before submitting their application
C Students must have completed at least 30 credit hours with a GPA of 2.75 or above ✓
D Students must have previously traveled to the country they wish to study in
Explanation: The email states: "you must have completed at least 30 credit hours and have a cumulative GPA of 2.75 or higher." This directly matches C.

A mentions foreign language study, which is never referenced. B is wrong because the information session is described as an opportunity, not a requirement. D describes prior travel experience, which is not mentioned anywhere.

Correct: B Your answer: —
A Paper and cardboard materials
B Plastic bottles, glass, and aluminum cans ✓
C Food waste and leftover liquids
D Non-recyclable waste and contaminated items
Explanation: The notice clearly states: "Green bin: Plastic bottles, glass, and aluminum cans" = B.

A describes the blue bin contents. C is explicitly excluded from all recycling bins. D describes the black bin contents. This is a straightforward detail-matching question.
Question 4 — Inference
What can be inferred about the university's current recycling system?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A It already achieves a recycling rate above 60 percent.
B It uses a single-bin system that does not effectively separate recyclable materials. ✓
C It was implemented less than one year ago and is still being tested.
D It requires students to transport their recyclables to an off-campus facility.
Explanation: The notice states that "the new system replaces the current single-bin collection" and the goal is to "increase its recycling rate from 35 percent to 60 percent." From this, we can infer that the current single-bin system is not effective at separating materials, which is why the rate is only 35% = B.

A is contradicted by the stated 35% rate. C has no support in the text. D mentions off-campus transport, which is never discussed.
