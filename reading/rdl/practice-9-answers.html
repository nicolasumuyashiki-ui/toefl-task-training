Read in Daily Life Strategy Tips
1 Student-to-professor emails: find the single "ask"
Polite request emails often open with pleasantries and background before getting to the point. Look for phrases like:

The ask: I was hoping you might... / Would you be willing to... / I would appreciate it if you could...
Background (skip for purpose Qs): Since I completed... / As you may recall...
Q1: The sentence "I was hoping you might be willing to write one on my behalf" (referring to a letter of recommendation) is the true purpose = A. The rest is context.
2 Reading forms: scan by section headers
Forms are organized into labeled sections (Applicant Info, Preferences, etc.). Don't read top-to-bottom — jump to the section named in the question.
Q3: The question asks about "first-choice placement" → go directly to the Placement Preference section → read the "1st Choice" field = B (Harwick Marine Institute — Field Research Assistant).
3 NOT / EXCEPT questions: check every option against the text
For negative factual questions, three of the four options ARE mentioned in the passage. Your job is to find the one that is NOT there.
Strategy: Check off each option as you locate it in the form. The remaining unchecked option is your answer.
Q4: Year ✓ (Junior), Major ✓ (Environmental Science), Submission date ✓ (03/06/2026). GPA is never listed anywhere = C.
4 Inference from checkboxes: combine the label with the check
A checkbox carries two pieces of info: the statement and whether it's selected. The inference usually comes from pairing them with nearby qualifying text (e.g., "housing stipend required").
Q5: "Willing to relocate ✓ (housing stipend required)" + "Available for unpaid placements ✗" → he can move, but he needs housing support = C.

Correct: A Your answer: —
A To ask the professor to write a letter of recommendation ✓
B To request feedback on a draft personal statement
C To apply directly for the Coastal Marine Biology program
D To schedule a meeting about summer research opportunities
Explanation: Daniel writes that the Harwick program "requires two letters of recommendation" and asks if the professor "might be willing to write one on my behalf." That is the core request = A.

B is a trap — Daniel offers to send his personal statement as a reference document, but does not ask for feedback on it. C is wrong because Daniel is applying himself; he isn't asking the professor to apply. D is never mentioned.

Question 5 — Inference
What can be inferred about the applicant from the checkbox selections?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A He will accept an internship in any location only if it pays
B He prefers local placements and cannot travel for the summer
C He is open to relocating but needs financial support for housing ✓
D He has already secured housing near both placement sites
Explanation: The first checkbox is selected: "Willing to relocate for the duration of the internship (housing stipend required)." The parenthetical ties his willingness to the need for financial housing support = C.

A is too strong ("only if it pays"); the "Available for unpaid placements" box is unchecked but that doesn't constrain location. B contradicts the checked relocation box. D introduces information not stated anywhere on the form.
