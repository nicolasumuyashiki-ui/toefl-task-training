Read in Daily Life Strategy Tips
1 Read forms like a table of facts
Forms store one fact per row. Match the question's key phrase to the left-hand label and read only that row's value. Most form questions are directly factual.
Q1: The question asks about "permit zone requested." Find that row: "Zone C — West Commuter Lot" = C.
2 Use small notes for what they imply
Forms often include a small Note or footnote. These are the source of inference questions — the note tells you what is and is not permitted.
Q2: The note says Zone C is valid only Mon–Fri, 7 AM–8 PM. Therefore, leaving the car on Sunday is not permitted = A.
3 Receipts: read the totals block carefully
A receipt question is usually about how a discount, tax, or fee was applied. Look at the label next to the amount, not just the number.
Q3: The line reads "Student discount (10%, books only)." That tells you both the rate and the scope = B.
Common trap: Q5 asks what can be inferred about sales tax. Tax is "6.5%, non-book items" — books were not taxed, which means textbooks are exempt = B.
Passage 1 Form
Question 1 — Factual Information
According to the form, which permit zone did the applicant request?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A Zone A, reserved for first-year resident students
B Zone B, the east lot reserved for faculty parking
C Zone C, the west commuter lot near campus ✓
D Zone D, the overnight lot for residential students
Explanation: The "Permit Zone Requested" row reads "Zone C — West Commuter Lot" = C.

A, B, and D invent other zones not listed on the form. Zones A, B, and D are plausible distractors but none of them appears in the application.
Question 2 — Inference
What can be inferred about the applicant's parking situation?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A He cannot leave his car in the lot on Sunday afternoons. ✓
B He will receive a full refund if the permit is unused.
C He must renew the permit each month throughout the semester.
D He plans to move into a campus residence hall soon.
Explanation: The note states Zone C permits are valid "only Monday through Friday." Sunday is not covered, so the applicant cannot use the lot on Sunday = A.

B is not mentioned. C is wrong because he selected the Full Semester option, not Monthly. D contradicts "Off-campus commuter" in the residence status field.
Passage 2 Receipt
Question 3 — Factual Information
According to the receipt, how was the student discount applied?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A As a flat ten-dollar reduction on the total price
B As a ten percent reduction, applied only to books ✓
C As a full refund of the six-point-five percent tax
D As store credit that can be used on future visits
Explanation: The receipt line reads "Student discount (10%, books only)" with a deduction of $24.55 = B.

A confuses a flat dollar amount with a percentage. C mixes up the tax line with the discount line. D is not mentioned — the discount was applied to this transaction, not saved as credit.
Question 4 — Negative Factual (EXCEPT)
According to the return policy, all of the following are true EXCEPT
Correct: B Your answer: —
A Returns are accepted within fourteen days with the receipt.
B Opened textbooks can be returned for a full cash refund. ✓
C Digital access codes cannot be refunded at any time.
D Defective electronics may be exchanged within seven days.
Explanation: The footer states "Opened textbooks and digital access codes are non-refundable." Therefore B is false and is the correct EXCEPT answer.

A, C, and D all match the footer text exactly: 14-day returns with receipt, non-refundable access codes, and 7-day defect exchange for electronics.
Question 5 — Inference
What can be inferred about the sales tax on this purchase?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A Tax was charged on every item listed on the receipt.
B Textbooks in this state are exempt from sales tax. ✓
C Tax was applied only after the student discount ended.
D The tax was refunded at the checkout counter.
Explanation: The tax line specifies "6.5%, non-book items." Because tax applies only to non-book items, textbooks must be tax-exempt in this state = B.

A contradicts the parenthetical scope. C invents a sequencing rule not stated. D is the opposite of what happened — tax was charged, not refunded.
