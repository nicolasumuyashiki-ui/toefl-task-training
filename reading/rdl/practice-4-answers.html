Read in Daily Life Strategy Tips
1 Identify the main purpose quickly
Read the subject line (email) or title (notice) first — it often reveals the main purpose immediately. Then scan the opening sentence of the body for confirmation.
Q1: The subject line says "New Meal Plan Options — Spring Semester" and the first sentence announces "two new meal plan options." This is clearly informing students about new choices = C.
2 Detail questions: find the exact sentence
For factual detail questions, locate the specific sentence in the passage that addresses the question. The correct answer will paraphrase that sentence. Do not rely on memory — always go back to the text.
Q2: The email says "Changes made after this date will not take effect until the following month." This directly matches answer B.
3 Inference: choose what the text supports, not what you assume
Inference answers must be logically supported by specific information in the passage. Avoid options that sound reasonable but have no textual basis.
Common trap: An option may sound like common sense, but if the passage does not mention it, it cannot be the answer. Stick to what is stated or directly implied.
Passage 1 Email
Question 1 — Main Purpose
What is the main purpose of this email?
Correct: C Your answer: —
A To require all students to purchase a new meal plan
B To explain the hours of operation for campus dining halls
C To inform students about new meal plan choices and how to switch plans ✓
D To announce that the campus cafeteria will be closed for renovations
Explanation: The email announces two new meal plan options (Flex Plan and Light Plan) and tells students how to switch plans through the student portal by January 20. The purpose is to inform students about new choices and the switching process = C.

A is wrong because the email says students "may now choose" — it is optional, not required. B is not mentioned anywhere in the email. D is completely unrelated to the email content.
Question 2 — Factual Detail
According to the email, what will happen if a student submits a meal plan change request after January 20?
Correct: B Your answer: —
A The request will be rejected and the student must wait until next semester.
B The change will not take effect until the following month. ✓
C The student will be automatically placed on the Flex Plan.
D The student will need to pay an additional processing fee.
Explanation: The email states: "Changes made after this date will not take effect until the following month." This directly supports B.

A is too extreme — the email does not say the request will be "rejected," only that it will be delayed. C has no basis in the email; no automatic assignment is mentioned. D mentions a fee that is not discussed anywhere in the email.
Passage 2 Notice
Question 3 — Factual Detail
According to the notice, which of the following is a change being introduced for the 2026-2027 year?
Correct: A Your answer: —
A A new discount for students who carpool with three or more people ✓
B Free parking for all students who live on campus
C A reduction in the price of all parking permits
D Extended business hours at the Transportation Services office
Explanation: The notice lists "A new carpool discount of 20 percent will be offered to groups of three or more students who register a shared vehicle." This matches A.

B is the opposite of what the notice says — freshman students will actually lose individual parking eligibility. C is wrong because permit fees are increasing by 10 percent, not decreasing. D is not mentioned; the business hours are stated as existing hours, not new ones.
Question 4 — Inference
What can be inferred about freshman students living on campus starting in 2026-2027?
Correct: D Your answer: —
A They will be required to use public transportation exclusively.
B They will not be able to park their own cars on campus individually.
C They will receive free bus passes as part of their housing fees.
D They will be exempt from the 10 percent fee increase. ✓
Explanation: The notice says freshman students in campus housing "will no longer be eligible for individual parking permits." Since they cannot purchase individual permits, the 10 percent fee increase on permits does not apply to them — they are effectively exempt = D.

A is too extreme — the notice does not say they must use public transportation "exclusively." B is close, but the notice mentions that "shared-vehicle permits will be available," meaning freshmen can still park on campus through a shared arrangement; they simply cannot get their own individual permit. C mentions free bus passes, which are never discussed in the notice.
