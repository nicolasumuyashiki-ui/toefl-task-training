========================================
Listen to part of a lecture in a biology class.

========================================
TALK 1 SCRIPT (talk1.mp3)
========================================
[MAN] Today I'd like to discuss something truly remarkable — how birds navigate during migration. Every year, millions of birds travel thousands of kilometers between their breeding and wintering grounds, and scientists have long wondered how they find their way so accurately. It turns out that birds rely on several different navigation tools. One of the most important is the Earth's magnetic field. Research has shown that certain birds have tiny particles of magnetite in their beaks, which act like a built-in compass. In addition to this magnetic sense, birds also use the position of the sun during the day and the stars at night to orient themselves. Perhaps most surprisingly, some species can even detect patterns of polarized light in the sky, which remain visible even on cloudy days. Young birds on their first migration often follow experienced adults, but experiments have demonstrated that many species are born with an innate sense of direction — they know which way to fly even without guidance.

NARRATOR (talk2-narrator.mp3)
========================================
Listen to part of a lecture in a history class.

========================================
TALK 2 SCRIPT (talk2.mp3)
========================================
[WOMAN] Let's turn our attention to one of the most transformative inventions in human history — the printing press. When Johannes Gutenberg developed his movable-type printing press around 1440, he probably had no idea how profoundly it would change European society. Before the printing press, books were copied by hand, mostly by monks in monasteries. This process was incredibly slow and expensive, which meant that books were rare luxury items that only the wealthy and the Church could afford. Gutenberg's invention changed all of that. Suddenly, it became possible to produce hundreds of copies of a book in the time it previously took to make just one. The first major impact was on literacy. As books became cheaper and more widely available, more people had the opportunity to learn to read. This was especially significant because, for the first time, knowledge was no longer controlled exclusively by religious institutions. The printing press also played a crucial role in the spread of new ideas during the Renaissance and the Reformation. Martin Luther's famous Ninety-Five Theses, for example, were printed and distributed across Europe within weeks — something that would have been impossible before Gutenberg. Scientists, too, benefited enormously. Researchers could now share their findings with colleagues in other countries quickly and accurately, which accelerated the pace of scientific discovery. However, not everyone welcomed the printing press. Some religious leaders feared that widespread access to books would lead to the spread of dangerous or heretical ideas. Despite this opposition, the printing press continued to transform society, laying the foundation for the modern information age.

  A) The biography of Johannes Gutenberg
  B) The role of monks in preserving knowledge
  C) The history of book manufacturing techniques
  D) How the printing press transformed European society
  Answer: D

Q6. According to the speaker, what happened to literacy after the printing press was introduced?
  A) It declined because books were too complicated
  B) It increased as books became more affordable
  C) It remained limited to religious institutions
  D) It was restricted by government regulations
  Answer: B

Q7. Why does the speaker mention Martin Luther's Ninety-Five Theses?
  A) To illustrate how the printing press helped spread new ideas
  B) To explain the origins of the Reformation
  C) To compare religious and scientific writing
  D) To describe Gutenberg's personal beliefs
  Answer: A

Q8. Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a result of the printing press?
  A) Faster sharing of scientific discoveries
  B) Increased literacy rates
  C) The establishment of public libraries
  D) Opposition from some religious leaders
  Answer: C
