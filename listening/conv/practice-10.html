Conversation 1

Listen to a conversation.
Setting: Campus Housing Office — Student (female) + Housing Coordinator (male)
Student: Hi, I was wondering if it's possible to switch to a different dormitory for the spring semester.
Coordinator: Sure, we do allow room transfers between semesters. Is there a particular reason you'd like to move?
Student: My current dorm is really far from the science buildings where most of my classes are. I spend almost thirty minutes walking each way.
Coordinator: I understand. Well, Morrison Hall and Lakeside Residence are both much closer to the science complex. Morrison has single rooms available, and Lakeside has doubles.
Student: I'd prefer a single if possible. What's the process for transferring?
Coordinator: You'll need to fill out a transfer request form, which I can give you now. The deadline to submit it is December 15th. After that, we'll process requests on a first-come, first-served basis.
Student: Is there any difference in cost between my current dorm and Morrison?
Coordinator: Singles in Morrison are about three hundred dollars more per semester than your current double. The difference would be added to your spring housing bill.
Student: That's reasonable. I'll take the form and submit it as soon as possible.

Q1. Why does the student want to change dormitories?

A) Her current roommate is too noisy.
B) Her dorm is too far from her classes.
C) She wants to live closer to the dining hall.
D) Her building is undergoing renovation.

Q2. What does the coordinator say about the transfer process?

A) Students must get approval from their current RA.
B) Transfers are only available for second-year students.
C) Requests are handled on a first-come, first-served basis.
D) The student must attend a housing orientation session.

Conversation 2

Listen to a conversation.
Setting: Campus Career Services Center — Student (male) + Career Advisor (female)
Student: Hi, I have a job interview next week and I've never done one before. I was hoping to get some advice.
Advisor: Of course! First of all, what kind of position is it?
Student: It's a summer internship at a marketing firm downtown. The interview is in person.
Advisor: Great. The most important thing is preparation. Research the company beforehand so you can explain why you want to work there specifically. Also, prepare two or three examples of projects or experiences that show relevant skills.
Student: What about the way I dress? I'm not sure how formal I should be.
Advisor: For a marketing firm, business casual is usually appropriate. A collared shirt and dress pants would be fine. Avoid jeans and sneakers.
Student: Got it. Is there anything else I should know?
Advisor: Yes, we offer mock interviews here at the career center. I'd strongly recommend signing up for one. It's a great way to practice answering questions and get feedback before the real thing.
Student: That sounds really helpful. Can I schedule one for this week?
Advisor: Absolutely. Let me check the calendar. We have openings on Wednesday and Friday afternoon.

Q3. What can be inferred about why the student visits the career center?

A) He feels unprepared for his first job interview.
B) He needs help writing his resume for the firm.
C) He wants to find a part-time job on campus.
D) He is unsure whether to accept the internship offer.

Q4. What will the student most likely do before the interview?

A) Visit the company's office in advance.
B) Send a thank-you email to the interviewer.
C) Complete an online interview training course.
D) Schedule a mock interview at the career center.
