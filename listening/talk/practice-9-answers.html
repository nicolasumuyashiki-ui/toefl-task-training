Talk 1 — The Bystander Effect (Social Science)

話者：教授 ｜ Standard

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in a psychology class.

Today we're going to look at a fascinating phenomenon in social psychology called the bystander effect. The bystander effect refers to the tendency for people to be less likely to help someone in need when other people are present. In other words, the more witnesses there are to an emergency, the less likely any single person is to step in and offer assistance.

This concept was first studied by psychologists John Darley and Bibb Latane in the late 1960s, following the widely reported case of Kitty Genovese, a woman who was attacked in New York City while numerous neighbors allegedly watched without intervening.

Darley and Latane proposed two main explanations for this behavior. The first is called diffusion of responsibility, which means that when others are present, each individual feels less personal obligation to act because they assume someone else will help. The second is pluralistic ignorance, where people look at the reactions of those around them to decide how to behave. If nobody else appears alarmed, each person concludes that the situation must not be serious.

Later research has shown that the bystander effect can be reduced in certain conditions, such as when bystanders believe they are the only one who can help, or when the victim directly asks a specific person for assistance.
1
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) The history of psychology as an academic discipline.
(B) How people behave differently in group settings during emergencies.
(C) Crime prevention strategies in urban areas.
(D) The relationship between personality and helping behavior.
解説： 冒頭で「Today we're going to look at a fascinating phenomenon ... called the bystander effect」と主題を明示。全体を通して緊急時のグループ行動について議論されている。(A)は心理学全般で範囲が広すぎ、(C)(D)は言及なし。
🔑 Main Idea問題：冒頭の「Today we're going to look at...」がそのまま答え。★シグナルワード直後に注目。
2
Why does the speaker mention the Kitty Genovese case?
Purpose
(A) To illustrate the effectiveness of neighborhood watch programs.
(B) To explain why crime rates increased in the 1960s.
(C) To argue that bystanders should be held legally responsible.
(D) To describe the event that inspired research on the bystander effect.
解説： Kitty Genovese事件は「following the widely reported case of...」と、DarleyとLataneが傍観者効果の研究を始めるきっかけとして紹介されている。(A)の近隣監視プログラム、(B)の犯罪率、(D)の法的責任は言及なし。
🔑 Purpose問題のコツ：「Why does the speaker mention X?」→ Xの前後の文脈を見る。ここでは「first studied ... following the ... case」と研究のきっかけとして提示されている。
3
According to the speaker, what is diffusion of responsibility?
Detail
(A) The belief that helping is not one's duty when others are present.
(B) The tendency to help more when in a group.
(C) The fear of being judged for intervening.
(D) The confusion about what kind of help to offer.
解説： 講師は「each individual feels less personal obligation to act because they assume someone else will help」と明確に定義。他者がいる場合に個人の義務感が薄れるという信念。(B)は正反対、(C)(D)は別の概念。
🔑 専門用語の定義問題：「called X」「known as X」と導入された直後に必ず説明が続く。「which means...」の後が定義そのもの。
4
What can reduce the bystander effect?
Detail
(A) Having more people present at the scene.
(B) Keeping the emergency quiet so fewer people notice.
(C) Waiting for trained professionals to arrive.
(D) Asking a specific person for help directly.
解説： 講師は「when the victim directly asks a specific person for assistance」と述べている。特定の人に直接助けを求めることで傍観者効果が減少する。(A)はむしろ効果を強める。(B)(D)は言及なし。
🔑 Detail問題：講義の最後に解決策が述べられることが多い。「can be reduced ... such as when...」のパターンに注目。

Talk 2 — The Water Cycle and Climate Change (Physical Science)

話者：教授 ｜ Harder

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in an earth science class.

Let's talk about the water cycle and how climate change is affecting this fundamental process. The water cycle, also known as the hydrological cycle, describes the continuous movement of water through the Earth's systems.

It begins with evaporation, when heat from the sun causes water from oceans, lakes, and rivers to change from liquid to vapor and rise into the atmosphere. Plants also contribute through a process called transpiration, where water is released from their leaves into the air. Once in the atmosphere, the water vapor cools and condenses to form clouds. This is called condensation.

When the water droplets in clouds become heavy enough, they fall back to the Earth's surface as precipitation — rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Some of this precipitation flows across the land surface as runoff and eventually returns to rivers, lakes, and the ocean. Some soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater, which can remain underground for thousands of years before eventually reaching the surface again.

Now, here's where climate change enters the picture. As global temperatures rise, the rate of evaporation increases because warmer air can hold more moisture. This means more water vapor enters the atmosphere, which leads to more intense precipitation events in some regions. However, the distribution is uneven. While some areas experience heavier rainfall and flooding, others face prolonged droughts because shifting weather patterns redirect moisture away from them.

Additionally, rising temperatures are causing glaciers and polar ice caps to melt at accelerated rates. This meltwater adds to ocean volumes, contributing to sea level rise, but it also reduces the freshwater reserves that billions of people depend on for drinking water and agriculture.

Scientists have found that the water cycle has intensified by roughly two percent per decade since the 1950s, and this trend is expected to continue as greenhouse gas concentrations increase.
5
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) The water cycle and how climate change is disrupting it.
(B) Methods of purifying drinking water.
(C) The formation of different types of clouds.
(D) Strategies for conserving water in agriculture.
解説： 冒頭で「Let's talk about the water cycle and how climate change is affecting this fundamental process」と主題を明示。前半で水循環の仕組み、後半で気候変動の影響を説明。(A)(C)(D)は主題ではない。
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Let's talk about...」が冒頭に来たら、その直後がトピック。2つの要素（水循環＋気候変動）を含む選択肢を選ぶ。
6
According to the speaker, what is transpiration?
Detail
(A) The process of water vapor turning into clouds.
(B) The release of water from plant leaves into the atmosphere.
(C) The movement of water underground.
(D) The melting of glaciers due to rising temperatures.
解説： 講師は「transpiration, where water is released from their leaves into the air」と明確に定義。(A)は凝結（condensation）、(C)は地下浸透、(D)は氷河融解で別の概念。
🔑 専門用語の定義問題：「called X, where...」のパターンで導入される。where以下が定義そのもの。
7
Why does the speaker say "the distribution is uneven"?
Purpose
(A) To explain why all regions receive the same amount of rain.
(B) To argue that climate change only affects coastal areas.
(C) To point out that some areas get more rain while others face droughts.
(D) To compare rainfall in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
解説： 「However, the distribution is uneven」の直後に「some areas experience heavier rainfall and flooding, others face prolonged droughts」と説明。降水量の地域差を強調している。(A)は正反対。
🔑 Purpose問題：「However」（⇔対比シグナル）の後に出てきた発言。前の内容（降水量増加）に対して「でも均一ではない」と対比を示す役割。
8
Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a consequence of the intensified water cycle?
NOT/EXCEPT
(A) More intense precipitation events.
(B) Melting of glaciers and polar ice caps.
(C) Increased earthquake activity.
(D) Prolonged droughts in some regions.
解説： 講師が挙げた影響は、より激しい降水現象(A)、氷河の融解(B)、長期的な干ばつ(D)。地震活動の増加(C)は言及されていない。NOT問題は消去法で3つの正しい情報を確認する。
🔑 NOT/EXCEPT問題の鉄則：消去法で解く。本文に言及のある3つを消し、残った1つが正解。メモで各ポイントにチェックを入れると確実。
