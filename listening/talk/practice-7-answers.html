Talk 1 — The Role of Sleep in Memory Consolidation (Life Science)

話者：教授 ｜ StandardSpeaker: Professor | Standard

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）View transcript (signal words highlighted)

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in a life science class.

Today we're going to look at something you all do every night but probably don't think much about — sleep, and specifically its role in memory consolidation.

When you learn something new during the day, that information is initially stored in the hippocampus, a region deep inside the brain. However, this storage is temporary and fragile. During sleep, particularly during the deep sleep stages known as slow-wave sleep, the brain replays the day's experiences. This process transfers memories from the hippocampus to the neocortex, where they become more stable and long-lasting.

Research by neuroscientist Matthew Walker has shown that students who sleep well after studying retain significantly more information than those who stay up all night cramming. In fact, even a short nap of twenty to thirty minutes can improve memory recall by up to twenty percent.

So the next time you're tempted to pull an all-nighter before an exam, remember that sleep might actually be your best study tool.

Talk 2 — The Industrial Revolution and Its Social Impact (History)

話者：教授 ｜ HarderSpeaker: Professor | Harder

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）View transcript (signal words highlighted)

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in a history class.

Let's turn our attention today to one of the most transformative periods in human history — the Industrial Revolution. Beginning in Britain in the late eighteenth century, the Industrial Revolution marked a shift from agricultural economies to ones based on manufacturing and industry.

The introduction of machines like the spinning jenny and the steam engine dramatically increased production capacity. Goods that once took weeks to produce by hand could now be manufactured in hours.

But the impact went far beyond economics. Socially, the revolution triggered one of the largest migrations in history. Millions of people left their rural villages and moved to cities in search of factory jobs. This rapid urbanization created enormous challenges. Cities like Manchester and Birmingham grew so quickly that housing, sanitation, and public health systems couldn't keep up.

Workers, including children as young as five, often labored for fourteen to sixteen hours a day in dangerous conditions for very low wages. These harsh realities eventually led to significant social reforms. Labor unions began to form, demanding better working conditions and fair pay. The Factory Acts, beginning in 1833, were among the first laws to limit child labor and regulate working hours. Education reform followed, as people recognized that an industrial economy required a more educated workforce.

So while the Industrial Revolution brought tremendous economic growth and technological progress, it also forced societies to confront serious questions about workers' rights, public welfare, and the responsibility of governments to protect their citizens.

(A) steam engines are partial; (B) labor unions are partial; (C) rural vs. urban is partial.
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Let's turn our attention to...the Industrial Revolution」で主題導入。Main Idea: 'Let's turn our attention to...the Industrial Revolution' introduces the theme.

(A) it's migration, not depopulation; (B) (D) are not mentioned.

A concrete example of government response to working-condition problems. (B) profitability; (C) machine invention; (D) UK-US comparison are not mentioned.

What can be inferred from the talk?
Inference
(A) The Industrial Revolution had only negative effects on society.
(B) Child labor was considered acceptable before the Factory Acts.
(C) Cities were well prepared for the influx of factory workers.
(D) Labor unions existed before the Industrial Revolution.
解説：Explanation: 「children as young as five...labored for fourteen to sixteen hours」とあり、Factory Actsが「first laws to limit child labor」であることから、それ以前は児童労働が容認されていたと推測できる。(A)経済成長もあった、(C)都市は対応できなかった、(D)労働組合は産業革命後に形成。Given 'children as young as five...labored for fourteen to sixteen hours' and that the Factory Acts were 'first laws to limit child labor,' we can infer child labor was tolerated before that. (A) there was also economic growth; (C) cities couldn't keep up; (D) labor unions formed after the Industrial Revolution.
🔑 Inference問題：「first laws to limit child labor」→ それ以前は法規制がなかった＝容認されていた。Inference: 'first laws to limit child labor' → no regulation existed before = it was tolerated.
