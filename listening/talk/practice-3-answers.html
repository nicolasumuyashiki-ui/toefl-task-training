Talk 1 — The Influence of Jazz on American Culture (Art & Music)

話者：教授 ｜ Standard

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）
Today we're going to look at how jazz music shaped American culture in the twentieth century. Jazz originated in the African American communities of New Orleans in the late 1800s, blending elements of blues, ragtime, and European harmonies.

What made jazz truly revolutionary was its emphasis on improvisation — musicians didn't just play written notes; they created new melodies on the spot, responding to each other in real time.

By the 1920s, jazz had spread across the country and became the soundtrack of what we call the Jazz Age. It wasn't just music — it influenced fashion, language, and social attitudes. Jazz clubs became spaces where racial barriers were, at least partially, broken down, as people of different backgrounds came together to enjoy the music.

Artists like Louis Armstrong and Duke Ellington became cultural icons whose influence extended far beyond music. Jazz also laid the groundwork for later genres, including rhythm and blues, rock and roll, and hip-hop. In many ways, jazz represents the spirit of American creativity — the idea that something beautiful can emerge from collaboration, spontaneity, and the willingness to take risks.
1
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) How jazz influenced American culture beyond music.
(B) The technical elements of jazz composition.
(C) A biography of Louis Armstrong.
(D) The decline of jazz in modern America.
解説： 講義全体がジャズがアメリカ文化（ファッション、言語、社会的態度、人種間の壁）にどう影響を与えたかを説明。(B)技術的要素は部分的、(C)伝記ではない、(D)衰退は言及なし。
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Today we're going to look at how jazz music shaped American culture」がそのまま答え。
2
According to the speaker, what made jazz revolutionary?
Detail
(A) Its use of electronic instruments.
(B) Its popularity among wealthy audiences.
(C) Its origins in European classical music.
(D) Its emphasis on improvisation.
解説： 講師は「What made jazz truly revolutionary was its emphasis on improvisation」と明確に述べている。(A)電子楽器は言及なし、(B)富裕層は言及なし、(C)ヨーロッパの和声は要素の一つだが革命的とされた点ではない。
🔑 Detail問題：「What made X truly revolutionary was...」の構文が答えを直接示す。
3
Why does the speaker mention jazz clubs?
Purpose
(A) To explain how musicians earned their income.
(B) To describe the architecture of the Jazz Age.
(C) To illustrate how jazz helped break down racial barriers.
(D) To compare jazz clubs with classical concert halls.
解説： 講師はジャズクラブについて「racial barriers were, at least partially, broken down, as people of different backgrounds came together」と述べている。人種的障壁の緩和の例として言及。
🔑 Purpose問題：ジャズクラブは社会的影響（人種の壁を越える場所）の具体例として挙げられている。
4
What can be inferred from the talk?
Inference
(A) Jazz was only popular during the 1920s.
(B) Modern music genres owe something to jazz.
(C) Jazz musicians always played from written scores.
(D) Jazz originated in Europe and spread to America.
解説： 講師は「Jazz also laid the groundwork for later genres, including rhythm and blues, rock and roll, and hip-hop」と述べている。現代の音楽ジャンルがジャズに負っていることが推論できる。(A)1920年代だけではない、(C)即興演奏が特徴、(D)ニューオーリンズで誕生。
🔑 Inference問題：「laid the groundwork for later genres」が現代音楽への影響を示唆。

Talk 2 — How Glaciers Shape Landscapes (Physical Science)

話者：教授 ｜ Harder

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）
Let's talk about one of the most powerful forces in shaping the Earth's surface — glaciers. A glacier is essentially a massive body of ice that forms over centuries as layers of snow compact and recrystallize. But glaciers are not just static ice formations; they move, and it is this movement that makes them such effective agents of landscape change.

Glaciers move through a combination of two processes. The first is internal deformation, where the weight of the ice causes it to slowly flow, almost like extremely thick liquid. The second process is basal sliding, where a thin layer of meltwater at the base of the glacier allows it to slide over the bedrock beneath.

As glaciers advance, they carry with them enormous amounts of rock and sediment, scraping and grinding the land beneath them. This process, called glacial erosion, creates distinctive landforms. For example, U-shaped valleys are carved by glaciers moving through mountain regions, in contrast to the V-shaped valleys created by rivers. Glaciers also create features called moraines, which are ridges of debris deposited along the edges and at the front of the glacier.

When a glacier retreats, it often leaves behind deep depressions that fill with water, forming glacial lakes. The Great Lakes of North America are perhaps the most famous example of lakes formed by glacial activity.

Scientists study glaciers not only to understand past geological events but also because glaciers serve as sensitive indicators of climate change. As global temperatures rise, glaciers around the world are retreating at an accelerating rate.
5
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) The formation of the Great Lakes.
(B) How glaciers move and shape the Earth's surface.
(C) The effects of climate change on polar ice caps.
(D) A comparison of different types of erosion.
解説： 講義全体が氷河の移動メカニズムと地形形成を説明。(A)五大湖は一例、(C)気候変動は末尾で触れるだけ、(D)浸食の比較は主旨ではない。
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Let's talk about...glaciers」で導入。移動と地形変化が二大テーマ。
6
According to the speaker, what are the two processes by which glaciers move?
Detail
(A) Evaporation and condensation.
(B) Tectonic shifting and volcanic activity.
(C) Internal deformation and basal sliding.
(D) Wind erosion and water erosion.
解説： 講師は「The first is internal deformation」「The second process is basal sliding」と2つのプロセスを明確に説明。(A)(B)(D)は氷河の移動メカニズムではない。
🔑 Detail問題：「The first is...The second process is...」のパターンで列挙される情報に注目。
7
Why does the speaker mention the Great Lakes?
Purpose
(A) To give an example of lakes formed by glacial activity.
(B) To explain why freshwater is becoming scarce.
(C) To compare lakes formed by rivers with those formed by glaciers.
(D) To describe the geography of North America.
解説： 講師は「The Great Lakes of North America are perhaps the most famous example of lakes formed by glacial activity」と述べており、氷河湖の具体例として言及。(B)淡水不足、(C)河川湖との比較、(D)北米地理は目的ではない。
🔑 Purpose問題：「the most famous example of...」が具体例の導入フレーズ。
8
Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a landform created by glaciers?
NOT/EXCEPT
(A) U-shaped valleys.
(B) Moraines.
(C) Glacial lakes.
(D) Sand dunes.
解説： 講師が挙げた氷河による地形はU字谷(A)、モレーン(B)、氷河湖(C)の3つ。砂丘(D)は氷河ではなく風によって形成されるもので、言及なし。
🔑 NOT/EXCEPT問題の鉄則：消去法で解く。本文に言及のある3つを消し、残った1つが正解。
