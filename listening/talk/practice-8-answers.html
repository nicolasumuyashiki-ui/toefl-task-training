Talk 1 — The Economics of Supply Chains (Business)

話者：教授 ｜ StandardSpeaker: Professor | Standard

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）View transcript (signal words highlighted)

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in a business class.

Today we're going to look at supply chain economics, which is essentially the study of how goods move from raw materials to finished products in the hands of consumers. A supply chain involves multiple stages: sourcing raw materials, manufacturing, warehousing, transportation, and finally retail distribution.

One of the key concepts here is efficiency. Companies are constantly trying to reduce costs at each stage. For example, many firms have adopted a strategy called just-in-time manufacturing, where parts and materials arrive at the factory exactly when they are needed, rather than being stored in expensive warehouses. This approach can significantly cut storage costs, but it also carries risks.

If a supplier fails to deliver on time, the entire production line can shut down. We saw this happen on a massive scale during the global shipping disruptions of the early 2020s, when delays at major ports caused shortages of everything from computer chips to household appliances.

So while lean supply chains are cost-effective under normal conditions, they can be extremely vulnerable to unexpected disruptions.

What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) How supply chains work and the trade-offs involved.
(B) The history of international trade agreements.
(C) How to start a manufacturing business.
(D) The role of government in regulating supply chains.
解説：Explanation: 冒頭で「supply chain economics」を紹介し、サプライチェーンの仕組みと効率化の利点・リスクを説明。(B)国際貿易協定、(C)起業、(D)政府の規制は言及なし。The opening introduces 'supply chain economics' and explains how supply chains work, plus the benefits and risks of efficiency. (B) international trade agreements; (C) entrepreneurship; (D) government regulation are not mentioned.
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Today we're going to look at...」の直後がトピック。最後の「So while...」が結論。Main Idea: the topic is right after 'Today we're going to look at...'. The conclusion is in the last 'So while...'.

(A) (C) (D) are not mentioned.

A concrete example showing the vulnerability of lean supply chains. (A) (B) (C) are not the purpose.
🔑 Purpose問題：「We saw this happen...」が前の一般論（リスク）の具体例であることを示す。Purpose: 'We saw this happen...' shows it's a concrete example of the previous generalization (the risk).

What can be inferred from the talk?
Inference
(A) Just-in-time manufacturing has been abandoned by most companies.
(B) Warehousing is no longer an important part of supply chains.
(C) Companies must balance cost savings with resilience in their supply chains.
(D) Supply chain disruptions only affect the technology industry.
解説：Explanation: 講師は最後に「while lean supply chains are cost-effective under normal conditions, they can be extremely vulnerable to unexpected disruptions」と結論。コスト効率とリスク耐性のバランスが必要。(A)放棄されたとは言っていない、(B)倉庫は不要ではない、(D)家電も影響を受けた。The lecturer concludes, 'while lean supply chains are cost-effective under normal conditions, they can be extremely vulnerable to unexpected disruptions.' Cost-efficiency and risk tolerance must be balanced. (A) it isn't said to be abandoned; (B) warehouses aren't unnecessary; (D) electronics were also affected.
🔑 Inference問題：「So while...」の結論文がバランスの必要性を示唆。Inference: the conclusion 'So while...' implies the need for balance.

Talk 2 — How Plants Communicate Through Chemical Signals (Life Science)

話者：教授 ｜ HarderSpeaker: Professor | Harder

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）View transcript (signal words highlighted)

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in a life science class.

When we think about communication, we usually think about animals — birds singing, dolphins clicking, or bees dancing. But plants communicate too, and they do it primarily through chemical signals.

Let me give you a well-known example. When a tobacco plant is attacked by caterpillars, it releases volatile organic compounds into the air. These airborne chemicals serve as a warning signal to neighboring tobacco plants, which then begin producing defensive toxins in their leaves before the caterpillars even reach them. In other words, the plants are essentially telling their neighbors that danger is approaching.

But it gets even more interesting than that. Some plants don't just warn their neighbors — they actually call for help. When corn plants are damaged by certain caterpillars, they release a specific blend of chemicals that attracts parasitic wasps. These wasps then lay their eggs inside the caterpillars, eventually killing them. So the plant is recruiting its own bodyguards through chemical messaging.

Plants also communicate underground through a network of fungal threads called mycorrhizal networks, sometimes referred to as the "wood wide web." Through these networks, trees can share nutrients with their neighbors and even send chemical distress signals when they are under attack.

Research by ecologist Suzanne Simard has shown that older trees, which she calls "mother trees," use these networks to channel nutrients to younger, smaller trees growing in the shade. This challenges the traditional view of forests as places of pure competition and suggests that cooperation plays an important role in plant communities as well.

What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) How plants defend themselves against disease.
(B) The role of fungi in forest ecosystems.
(C) How plants use chemical signals to communicate.
(D) A comparison of plant and animal communication.
解説：Explanation: 冒頭で「plants communicate too, and they do it primarily through chemical signals」と述べ、空気中の化学物質、寄生バチの誘引、菌根ネットワークなど複数の例を挙げている。(A)病気防御は部分的、(B)菌類は部分的、(D)動物との比較は導入のみ。The opening says, 'plants communicate too, and they do it primarily through chemical signals,' citing several examples — airborne chemicals, attracting parasitic wasps, mycorrhizal networks. (A) disease defense is partial; (B) fungi are partial; (D) animal comparison is only an introduction.
🔑 Main Idea問題：「But plants communicate too...through chemical signals」がトピック宣言。Main Idea: 'But plants communicate too...through chemical signals' is the topic statement.

According to the speaker, what are mycorrhizal networks?
Detail
(A) Underground fungal connections that link plants together.
(B) Root systems that absorb water from the soil.
(C) A type of chemical compound released by trees.
(D) Pathways used by insects to travel between plants.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「a network of fungal threads called mycorrhizal networks」と定義し、「Through these networks, trees can share nutrients with their neighbors and even send chemical distress signals」と説明。(B)水吸収の根系ではない、(C)化学物質ではなく菌糸、(D)昆虫の経路ではない。The lecturer defines 'a network of fungal threads called mycorrhizal networks' and explains, 'Through these networks, trees can share nutrients with their neighbors and even send chemical distress signals.' (B) it's not water-uptake roots; (C) it's fungal threads, not chemicals; (D) it's not insect routes.
🔑 定義問題：「called X」で導入された後の説明が定義。「a network of fungal threads」がキーフレーズ。Definition: after the introduction 'called X,' the explanation is the definition. 'a network of fungal threads' is the key phrase.

What does Suzanne Simard's research suggest about forests?
Inference
(A) Trees compete more aggressively than previously believed.
(B) Cooperation among trees is more significant than traditionally thought.
(C) Younger trees grow faster without the presence of older trees.
(D) Fungal networks are harmful to forest health.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「This challenges the traditional view of forests as places of pure competition and suggests that cooperation plays an important role in plant communities」と述べている。従来の競争一辺倒の見方に対し、協力の重要性が示唆。(A)より攻撃的は逆、(C)母木が若い木に栄養を送る、(D)有害ではない。The lecturer says, 'This challenges the traditional view of forests as places of pure competition and suggests that cooperation plays an important role in plant communities.' Implies cooperation is important — challenging the competition-only view. (A) more aggressive is the opposite; (C) mother trees send nutrients to young trees; (D) it's not harmful.
🔑 Inference問題：「challenges the traditional view」が既存の考え方を覆す新発見であることを示す。Inference: 'challenges the traditional view' shows it's a new finding overturning the existing belief.
