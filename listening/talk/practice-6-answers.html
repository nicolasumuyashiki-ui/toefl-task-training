Talk 1 — The Development of Perspective in Renaissance Art (Art & Music)

話者：教授 ｜ Standard

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in an art class.

Today we're going to look at one of the most important innovations in the history of Western art — the development of linear perspective during the Renaissance.

Before the fifteenth century, European paintings appeared flat. Figures were often shown the same size regardless of their position in the scene. This changed dramatically when the Italian architect Filippo Brunelleschi conducted a series of experiments around 1415, demonstrating how three-dimensional space could be represented on a two-dimensional surface using mathematical principles.

Shortly after, the painter Masaccio applied these ideas in his famous fresco The Holy Trinity, creating a remarkably convincing illusion of depth. The key technique involves establishing a single vanishing point on the horizon line, toward which all parallel lines in the scene converge.

This gave artists the ability to create realistic spatial relationships between objects and figures. The impact was enormous — perspective became the foundation of European painting for the next five hundred years.
1
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) The biography of Filippo Brunelleschi.
(B) The development of linear perspective in Renaissance art.
(C) A comparison of Renaissance and modern art styles.
(D) The history of Italian architecture.
解説： 冒頭で「the development of linear perspective during the Renaissance」と主題を明示。(A)ブルネレスキは一例、(C)現代美術との比較はなし、(D)イタリア建築の歴史ではない。
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Today we're going to look at...」の直後がトピック。
2
According to the speaker, what was European painting like before the fifteenth century?
Detail
(A) It appeared flat without realistic depth.
(B) It used bright colors but lacked detail.
(C) It focused mainly on landscapes rather than people.
(D) It was heavily influenced by Asian art techniques.
解説： 講師は「Before the fifteenth century, European paintings appeared flat」と明確に述べている。(B)(C)(D)は言及なし。
🔑 Detail問題：「Before the fifteenth century」の時間シグナルに注目。変化前の状態が問われている。
3
Why does the speaker mention Masaccio?
Purpose
(A) To explain who invented the vanishing point technique.
(B) To contrast his work with Brunelleschi's architecture.
(C) To argue that painting is more important than architecture.
(D) To give an example of an artist who applied perspective in painting.
解説： マザッチョはブルネレスキの遠近法の原理を実際の絵画に適用した画家として紹介されている。具体例としての役割。(A)消失点を発明したのはブルネレスキ、(B)(C)は比較していない。
🔑 Purpose問題：「Shortly after, the painter Masaccio applied these ideas」が具体例の導入。
4
What can be inferred from the talk?
Inference
(A) Brunelleschi's experiments had no lasting influence on art.
(B) Linear perspective was already common before the Renaissance.
(C) The development of perspective was a major turning point in Western art.
(D) Masaccio rejected the use of mathematical principles in his work.
解説： 講師は「perspective became the foundation of European painting for the next five hundred years」と述べている。遠近法が西洋美術の大きな転換点であったことが推論できる。(A)500年間の基礎になった、(B)ルネサンス以前は平面的、(D)マザッチョは原理を適用した。
🔑 Inference問題：最後の文「the foundation...for the next five hundred years」が長期的影響を示唆。

Talk 2 — How Language Shapes Thought (Social Science)

話者：教授 ｜ Harder

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）

Narrator: Listen to part of a lecture in a linguistics class.

I'd like to discuss a fascinating question that has occupied linguists and psychologists for decades: does the language you speak affect the way you think? This idea is known as the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis.

Now, there are two versions of this hypothesis. The strong version claims that language determines thought — meaning you literally cannot think about concepts that your language has no words for. Most modern researchers reject this extreme view. However, the weak version, known as linguistic relativity, suggests that language influences thought and perception without completely determining them. And there is growing evidence to support this weaker claim.

Consider color perception, for example. The Russian language has separate basic words for light blue and dark blue, treating them as fundamentally different colors. Studies have shown that Russian speakers can actually distinguish between shades of blue faster than English speakers.

Another striking example comes from the Kuuk Thaayorre people of Australia, who do not use words like left and right. Instead, they describe spatial relationships using cardinal directions. Research has found that speakers of this language have an extraordinary sense of orientation.

These examples suggest that while language does not imprison our thoughts, it does provide a kind of lens through which we interpret the world. The words available to us can highlight certain distinctions and make them easier to process, shaping our everyday experience in subtle but measurable ways.
5
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) The history of the Russian language.
(B) How language can influence the way people think.
(C) Why some languages are more complex than others.
(D) The differences between linguistics and psychology.
解説： 講義全体が「does the language you speak affect the way you think?」という問いを中心に展開。(A)ロシア語は一例、(C)言語の複雑さの比較はなし、(D)学問分野の違いは主題ではない。
🔑 Main Idea問題：冒頭の問いかけがそのまま主題を示す。
6
According to the speaker, what do most modern researchers think about the strong version of the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis?
Detail
(A) They reject it as too extreme.
(B) They fully support it with new evidence.
(C) They have never tested it experimentally.
(D) They consider it more valid than the weak version.
解説： 講師は「Most modern researchers reject this extreme view」と明確に述べている。(B)支持ではなく拒否、(C)実験なしとは述べていない、(D)弱い版の方が支持されている。
🔑 Detail問題：「Most modern researchers reject...」が直接の答え。
7
Why does the speaker mention the Kuuk Thaayorre people?
Purpose
(A) To show that some languages have no spatial vocabulary.
(B) To argue that all Indigenous languages are more precise than English.
(C) To explain why cardinal directions are rarely used in modern languages.
(D) To provide evidence that language can influence spatial awareness.
解説： クーク・ターヨール族は「左右」ではなく方角を使い、優れた方向感覚を持つ。言語が空間認識に影響する証拠として紹介。(A)空間語彙がないのではなく異なる種類、(B)全先住民言語とは言っていない、(C)説明の目的ではない。
🔑 Purpose問題：「Another striking example」のシグナルが二番目の証拠であることを示す。
8
Which of the following best describes the speaker's conclusion?
Inference
(A) Language completely controls how people perceive the world.
(B) Language has no measurable effect on thought processes.
(C) Language provides a lens that subtly shapes how we interpret experience.
(D) Only languages with large vocabularies can express complex ideas.
解説： 講師は最後に「language does not imprison our thoughts」しかし「it does provide a kind of lens through which we interpret the world」と述べている。完全支配ではないが微妙な影響がある。(A)「completely」は強い版で否定済み、(B)「no effect」は結論と矛盾、(D)は言及なし。
🔑 結論問題：最後のパラグラフに全集中。「does not imprison...but does provide a lens」が結論の核心。
