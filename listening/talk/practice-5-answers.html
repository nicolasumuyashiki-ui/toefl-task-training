Talk 1 — Supply and Demand in the Housing Market (Business)

話者：教授 ｜ StandardSpeaker: Professor | Standard

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）View transcript (signal words highlighted)
Today we're going to look at how the basic principles of supply and demand apply to the housing market. When a city experiences rapid population growth, demand for housing increases significantly. If builders cannot construct new homes fast enough to meet this demand, prices rise. This is exactly what we've seen in many major cities over the past decade.

On the other hand, when interest rates go up, borrowing money becomes more expensive. Fewer people can afford to take out mortgages, so demand drops and prices tend to stabilize or even decrease.

Government policies also play a role. For example, zoning regulations can limit the supply of new housing by restricting where and what type of buildings can be constructed. Some cities have introduced rent control policies to make housing more affordable, but economists often debate whether these policies actually help or create additional problems, such as reduced investment in new construction.

Understanding these dynamics is essential for anyone interested in real estate, urban planning, or public policy.
1
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) The history of urban development in major cities.
(B) How supply and demand affect housing prices.
(C) The advantages of renting versus buying a home.
(D) How to invest in real estate successfully.
解説：Explanation: 冒頭で「how the basic principles of supply and demand apply to the housing market」と主題を明示。人口増加・金利・政府政策が住宅価格にどう影響するかを説明。(A)都市開発の歴史、(C)賃貸vs購入、(D)不動産投資は言及なし。The opening declares the topic 'how the basic principles of supply and demand apply to the housing market.' Population growth, interest rates, and government policy are explained as factors. (A) urban-development history; (C) rent vs. buy; (D) real estate investment are not mentioned.
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Today we're going to look at...」の直後がトピック。Main Idea: the topic is right after 'Today we're going to look at...'.

According to the speaker, what happens when interest rates increase?
Detail
(A) More people buy houses because they want to lock in rates.
(B) The government introduces new zoning regulations.
(C) Fewer people can afford mortgages, so demand decreases.
(D) Builders increase the pace of new construction.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「when interest rates go up, borrowing money becomes more expensive. Fewer people can afford to take out mortgages, so demand drops」と明確に説明。(A)は逆の効果、(B)(D)は言及なし。The lecturer explains clearly, 'when interest rates go up, borrowing money becomes more expensive. Fewer people can afford to take out mortgages, so demand drops.' (A) is the opposite effect; (B) (D) are not mentioned.
🔑 Detail問題：「On the other hand」の対比シグナルの後に金利上昇の影響が述べられている。Detail: the impact of rising interest rates follows the contrast signal 'On the other hand.'

Why does the speaker mention zoning regulations?
Purpose
(A) To give an example of how government policy can limit housing supply.
(B) To explain why cities have grown rapidly.
(C) To argue that all government regulations are harmful.
(D) To describe the construction process for new buildings.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「Government policies also play a role」と導入し、ゾーニング規制を「limit the supply of new housing」の具体例として挙げている。「For example」がシグナル。The lecturer introduces 'Government policies also play a role,' citing zoning regulations as a concrete example of 'limit the supply of new housing.' 'For example' is the signal.
🔑 Purpose問題：「For example」のシグナル＝前の一般論（政府政策の影響）の具体例。Purpose: signal 'For example' = a concrete instance of the previous generalization (impact of government policy).
4
What does the speaker imply about rent control policies?
Inference
(A) They always succeed in making housing affordable.
(B) Their effectiveness is debated among economists.
(C) They have been universally rejected by economists.
(D) They only apply to commercial buildings.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「economists often debate whether these policies actually help or create additional problems」と述べている。効果について意見が分かれていることを示唆。(A)「always succeed」は極端、(C)「universally rejected」でもない、(D)は言及なし。The lecturer says, 'economists often debate whether these policies actually help or create additional problems.' Implies opinions are divided on the effect. (A) 'always succeed' is too extreme; (C) not 'universally rejected'; (D) is not mentioned.
🔑 Inference問題：「debate whether...help or create additional problems」は効果が不確実であることを示唆。Inference: 'debate whether...help or create additional problems' implies the effect is uncertain.

Talk 2 — The Life Cycle of Butterflies (Life Science)

話者：教授 ｜ StandardSpeaker: Professor | Standard

スクリプトを見る（シグナルワードをハイライト付き）View transcript (signal words highlighted)
Let's talk about one of nature's most remarkable transformations — the life cycle of butterflies. Butterflies undergo a process called complete metamorphosis, which consists of four distinct stages: egg, larva, pupa, and adult.

The cycle begins when a female butterfly lays her eggs on a host plant. She carefully selects the plant because the larvae, commonly known as caterpillars, will feed on its leaves as soon as they hatch. During the larval stage, the caterpillar's primary job is to eat and grow. It sheds its skin, or molts, several times as it increases in size.

Once the caterpillar has reached its full size, it enters the pupal stage. It forms a protective casing called a chrysalis. Inside the chrysalis, something truly extraordinary happens. The caterpillar's body essentially dissolves and reorganizes itself into the structure of an adult butterfly. This process can take anywhere from ten days to several months.

When the butterfly finally emerges from the chrysalis, it must wait for its wings to dry and harden before it can fly. The adult butterfly then focuses on two main activities: finding food, usually nectar from flowers, and reproducing. Some species, like the monarch butterfly, are famous for their long-distance migrations, traveling thousands of kilometers to reach warmer climates for the winter.
5
What is the talk mainly about?
Main Idea
(A) The migration patterns of monarch butterflies.
(B) How butterflies pollinate flowers.
(C) The differences between butterflies and moths.
(D) The four stages of butterfly metamorphosis.
解説：Explanation: 冒頭で「the life cycle of butterflies」を紹介し、卵・幼虫・蛹・成虫の4段階を順に説明。(A)モナルクチョウは末尾で簡単に触れるだけ、(B)花粉媒介は言及なし、(C)ガとの違いは未議論。The opening introduces 'the life cycle of butterflies' and explains the four stages — egg, larva, pupa, adult — in order. (A) the monarch is only briefly noted at the end; (B) pollination isn't mentioned; (C) differences from moths aren't discussed.
🔑 Main Idea問題：「Let's talk about...」で導入。4段階の説明が全体構造。Main Idea: introduced by 'Let's talk about...'. The four-stage explanation is the overall structure.
6
Why does the female butterfly carefully choose a host plant?
Detail
(A) Because the caterpillars will feed on its leaves after hatching.
(B) Because she needs the plant's nectar for energy.
(C) Because the plant provides protection from predators.
(D) Because the color of the plant attracts mates.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「She carefully selects the plant because the larvae...will feed on its leaves as soon as they hatch」と明確に理由を述べている。(B)成虫の蜜は別の話、(C)(D)は言及なし。The lecturer says clearly, 'She carefully selects the plant because the larvae...will feed on its leaves as soon as they hatch.' (B) adult nectar is a different matter; (C) (D) are not mentioned.
🔑 Detail問題：「because」のシグナルが理由を直接示す。Detail: signal 'because' shows the reason directly.
7
What happens inside the chrysalis?
Detail
(A) The caterpillar sleeps until spring arrives.
(B) The caterpillar continues to grow larger.
(C) The caterpillar produces silk threads for a cocoon.
(D) The caterpillar's body dissolves and reorganizes into an adult butterfly.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「The caterpillar's body essentially dissolves and reorganizes itself into the structure of an adult butterfly」と述べている。(A)春まで眠るのではない、(B)大きくなるのではなく完全変態、(C)絹糸はガの特徴。The lecturer says, 'The caterpillar's body essentially dissolves and reorganizes itself into the structure of an adult butterfly.' (A) it doesn't sleep until spring; (B) it doesn't just grow — it fully metamorphoses; (C) silk is a moth feature.
🔑 Detail問題：「something truly extraordinary happens」のシグナルの後に重要情報が続く。Detail: 'something truly extraordinary happens' is followed by key information.
8
Why does the speaker mention monarch butterflies?
Purpose
(A) To explain why butterflies are endangered.
(B) To describe the most common butterfly species.
(C) To give an example of butterflies known for long-distance migration.
(D) To compare the life cycles of different insect species.
解説：Explanation: 講師は「Some species, like the monarch butterfly, are famous for their long-distance migrations」とモナルクチョウを長距離移動の具体例として言及。(A)絶滅危機、(B)最も一般的、(D)比較は言及なし。The lecturer cites the monarch butterfly as a concrete example of long-distance migration: 'Some species, like the monarch butterfly, are famous for their long-distance migrations.' (A) extinction risk; (B) most common; (D) comparison are not mentioned.
🔑 Purpose問題：「like the monarch butterfly」が具体例の導入。前の一般論（成虫の行動）の補足例。Purpose: 'like the monarch butterfly' introduces a concrete example. A supplementary example to the previous generalization (adult behavior).
